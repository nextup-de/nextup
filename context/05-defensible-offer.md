# Fix the truth, then build the proof

NextUp's Harvey-ball feature analysis is **mostly an illusion today and only half real after PILOT**. Checked against the code on `origin/main` (`be913f4`, 2 Nov 2026), the "today" column overstates NextUp on all ten rows. The three headline mechanisms do not run for a real company. The 5-day clock is frozen at day 0, so the deputy never takes over and the +30/+90 measurement checks never open. The "never model output" score takes +20 points from an LLM routing pick and offers model-written text only when it raises the score. The "works-council-ready" read path sends every browser the full event log, private cases and the real names of the anonymous members. Rated on code evidence, NextUp's unweighted total falls from **24 to 13 today** and from **34 to 23 after the whole PILOT plan**. That puts it level with Qmarkets and below HYPE (about 30), rready (about 29) and target (about 24) once their own ratings are corrected. Those corrections mostly go up: deadlines and escalation exist at Ideanote, target and HYPE, and the classic German BVW already pays premiums on realised first-year savings. After PILOT, NextUp still lacks the expected BVW plumbing: a Gutachter and committee step, premium calculation with payroll export, ZI KPIs, translation of idea content, ISO 27001 and a written SLA. It also has no customer, no signed works agreement and no measured outcome. What can still set NextUp apart is narrow and real. It can show the submitter an answer date and hand the case to the deputy automatically. It can keep model output out of the score entirely, with every point citing a fact. It can make "no person ranking" a design rule, sell the works-council and AI-Act pack as a product, and build a cross-customer outcome dataset. None of these is worth anything until it is true in code, signed at one named customer and measured there. Investors will price NextUp as a system of record for improvement decisions only after a paid PoC, an LOI and one reference with measured results. Idea management alone is a PE-consolidated niche of roughly €30M at its leader.

## After PILOT, the expected offer still fails a German BVW tender

Kotler's levels explain the order in which a buyer judges. The expected layer is checked first, and NextUp's differentiators count only after it passes. The finished PILOT plan still leaves six expected-offer gaps. Tenders ask for a committee decision on the **Gutachter's** assessment and for "calculation of the premium via the software" ([softguide tender ~800](https://www.softguide.de/ausschreibungen/industrieunternehmen-sucht-software-fuer-das-ideenmanagement-ca-800)). A consumer-goods tender adds reminder mails, DE/EN/FR and premium handling ([softguide 18/1239](https://www.softguide.de/ausschreibungen/renommiertes-produktions-und-handelsunternehmen-sucht-ideenmanagement)). target sets the SAP benchmark: rewards go straight to payroll, with more than 50 preconfigured KPIs ([TIMETOACT](https://www.timetoact-group.com/en/details/target-idea-management-on-premise)). NextUp after PILOT has no reviewer role, assessment form or committee step. It has no premium formula, premium letter or payroll export. Its KPIs are answer times only, with no participation rate, ideas per 100 employees, implementation rate, savings or premiums. It translates the interface but not idea content. It has a VDA ISA self-assessment but no **ISO 27001**, which ITONICS has held since 2017 ([ITONICS Trust](https://www.itonics-innovation.com/trust)). And it has a support runbook but no **written SLA**. None of these is large in code. Together they decide whether NextUp gets past the first round.

The trust gaps run deeper, because they cannot be closed by building. After PILOT, NextUp still has no legal entity, no signed DPA, no pentest, no load test or users-per-stack figure, no real-tenant M365 or Jira run, and no customer installation. Its brain is sized for ten users ([claims vs code, claim 6](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/research_notes/NextUp%20defensible%20offer%20plan/claims_vs_code.md)). Measured impact remains a number typed by the person who benefits from it. The score remains a keyword formula that the product itself teaches people to game. GDP, which carries export, deletion, retention and the admin audit, is still at stage "idea" in the master plan ([PILOT.md:22](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/projects/nextup/hauptplaene/PILOT.md)). Kevin writes about 84% of the commits, so the bus factor is about one. The legal context makes these gaps decisive rather than cosmetic. Works councils co-determine the principles of the BVW ([WEKA](https://www.weka.de/betriebsrat-personalrat/ideenmanagement-so-bestimmt-der-betriebsrat-mit/)), and any software merely *able* to monitor performance triggers § 87(1) Nr. 6 BetrVG ([Luther](https://www.luther-lawfirm.com/en/newsroom/blog/detail/dauerbrenner-software-vs-mitbestimmung-87-abs-1-nr-6-betrvg)). A browser holding every actor id is exactly that capability.

## Five differentiators survive scrutiny, and none is a moat yet

Strip out what rivals already have, and five claims remain that no competitor publishes. The first is an **answer date shown to the submitter, with automatic hand-over to a deputy on breach**. The machinery exists everywhere, but no vendor states the promise or transfers ownership. The second is **a score that contains no model output at all, where every point cites the sentence it came from**. Human scorecards are standard, but a deterministic automatic pre-score with cited facts is not. The sharpest contrast is rready's "AI automates evaluation, routing, and reward allocation" ([rready BVW](https://rready.com/bvw)) and HYPE's AI savings estimates that feed reward calculations ([HYPE BVW](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/solutions/employee-suggestion-bvw)). Both put AI into money decisions that the works council co-determines. The third is **no person ranking, by design**. Ideanote ranks top ideators and teams, and nobody publishes a no-ranking rule. The fourth is **a works-council and AI-Act pack sold as a product**: model works-agreement annex, DPIA, Art. 26(7) information templates, per-decision explanation export and a read-only works-council/DPO role. No vendor publishes a model agreement, and the deployer must inform worker representatives before using a high-risk system at work ([AI Act Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)). The fifth is **an outcome dataset across customers**: answer times per decision type, share answered on time, and verified before/after results, which become benchmarks at about three to five customers.

None of these is hard to imitate on its own. Incumbents can configure the first three in one or two releases, and the counterchecks rate the answer promise "a positioning lead, not a technical moat" ([competitor counterchecks §1](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/research_notes/NextUp%20defensible%20offer%20plan/competitor_counterchecks.md)). What makes them defensible is what accumulates behind them. **Routing accuracy**: log the proposed and final route for every case, so each company's routing map improves. **Score calibration**: fit the weights to decisions taken and to +90-day verdicts, so the moat is the calibration, not the regex. **The answer-time ledger**: competitors can copy a timer but cannot copy a year of a customer's measured answer times. **A signed works-agreement annex** at a named account, which a rival would have to renegotiate. HYPE has a strategic brake here: its relaunch promises AI that will "project the business outcomes of innovations" ([HYPE relaunch](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-innovation-announces-strategic-relaunch)), so copying "AI never scores" would contradict its own pitch. The real long-term threat is not a startup. None was found in the AI-native BVW space. It is DIY: a Copilot or Power Apps intake, or a Joule Studio agent next to target inside SAP ([Microsoft Idea Coach](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/extensibility/agent-builder-template-idea-coach)). NextUp's answer to DIY is the accountability contract and works-council defaults, which internal builds lack.

## Investors need a category bigger than idea management

Idea management alone does not support a venture outcome. HYPE and Planbox merged under the buy-and-build investor Main Capital with a targeted combined turnover of **almost €30M**, as one of the world's largest players ([Main Capital](https://main.nl/?p=9896)). That is a PE roll-up market. Investors in 2025–2026 also discount thin wrappers and want a wedge into a system of record or a "system of action" that competes for labor budgets ([Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/roadmap-ai-systems-of-action)). Bessemer also warns that AI cuts integration cost by up to 90%, so integrations are necessary for adoption but weak as a moat. NextUp should therefore present itself to investors as **the system of record for improvement decisions in operations**: who decided, by when, on what evidence, with what measured result, in a form the works council has signed. It replaces CI-team and committee hours, not just HYPE licences. Idea capture stays the wedge and the buyer-facing name. Two exit analogues fit this framing. Peakon was sold to Workday for about **$700M** for its continuous employee-voice data ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/workday-acquires-peakon-for-700-million-to-capture-real-time-employee-sentiment-data/)). Tulip's **$120M Series D at $1.3B** was led by the strategic Mitsubishi Electric ([BuiltIn Boston](https://www.builtinboston.com/articles/tulip-raises-120m-series-d-1b-valuation-20260116)). The strategic exit buyers would be SAP, Siemens, PTC or QMS vendors. Main/HYPE is the floor, not the target (inference).

The round benchmarks set a realistic bar. The European pre-seed median pre-money was **€2.4M** in Q1 2025 ([PitchBook](https://files.pitchbook.com/website/files/pdf/Q1_2025_European_VC_Valuations_Report_19303.pdf)) and **$3.48M** in Q1 2026 ([Equidam](https://www.equidam.com/startup-valuation-delta-q1-2026/)). The seed median was about **€5.6M** ([waveup/PitchBook](https://waveup.com/blog/seed-round-benchmarks/)). Competitive European AI seeds run **€2–4M** ([unicorn-nest](https://unicorn-nest.com/media/analytics/ai-genai-seed-metrics-2026/)). Some investors now ask for **£500k+ ARR** at seed ([Kings eLab](https://www.kingselab.org/blog/philip-clements)), and the AI-era Series A bar is **$2–3M ARR** ([waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/series-a-benchmarks/)). A pre-revenue student team should therefore target a pre-seed of about €0.5–1.5M at €3–6M pre-money (estimate), and raise it only after three milestones. The first is a **paid PoC** (€20–60k, estimate) with a named Tier-1 supplier or OEM plant. A mid-size supplier is more realistic than BMW, which already runs rready. The second is an **LOI** with a written path to a frame contract. The third is **one reference with measured outcomes**: a baseline before go-live, at least 70% of cases answered within 5 days, a works-council sign-off and a written case study. One such signal outweighs many free pilots.

Non-dilutive money can carry the team to those milestones. ## Conclusion

The Harvey-ball chart measured NextUp's intentions against its rivals' brochures, and both sides were misread. The more useful finding is where the remaining advantage lies. It is not in features, which NextUp's own orch speed shows anyone can build in weeks. It is in **commitments that a works council can sign and a controller can verify**. Those become an asset only once they are true in code, written into one customer's agreement and backed by measured numbers. The short-term priority is therefore unusual for a startup: make the product say less, make the code match what it says, and spend the PILOT window closing the boring BVW plumbing that gets NextUp through a tender.

The strategic implication is that NextUp's investable story and its buyer story are different and should stay different. The buyer, a BVW or KVP lead, gets a faster, fairer idea process that plugs into SAP payroll. The investor gets the only decision-and-outcome record in operations that a German works council has approved, with a benchmark dataset growing behind it. Both stories depend on the same first proof point: one paid pilot whose measured answer times and outcomes NextUp is contractually allowed to publish.
