# PESTLE: legal and environmental factors

Text for the Miro PESTLE frames (2 Nov 2026). Legal is sorted by local, national and international level;
environmental is strictly ecological (cost pressure and job cuts belong under Economic and Social).

## Legal factors

*Rules NextUp and its customers must comply with, at local, national and international level.*

- **Local (the customer's plant):** the works agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung) sets how ideas are handled, judged and
  rewarded, and what the software may record.
- **National (Germany):**
  - **BetrVG §87:** the works council has a say over any suggestion scheme (No. 12) and any software that could
    monitor people (No. 6).
  - **Employee data and inventions:** §26 BDSG governs employee data. The Employee Inventions Act (ArbnErfG) applies
    to inventions.
  - **Trade secrets:** protected only with documented secrecy measures (GeschGehG).
- **International (EU):**
  - **GDPR:** a DPIA, a data processing agreement, and the rights to export and delete.
  - **EU AI Act:** AI that evaluates people is high-risk, with duties from Dec 2027.
  - **Copyright:** protects code, not functionality.
- **Industry:** car makers require TISAX or ISO 27001 before they buy.

### Scenarios

1. **As planned:** the AI Act applies from Dec 2027. Works councils approve tools that come with a clear
   works-agreement pack.
2. **Stricter:** idea scores count as performance monitoring, or NextUp is classed as high-risk AI. Approvals get
   longer and documentation duties grow.
3. **Looser:** the EU loosens or delays AI rules further. Approvals get faster, but rivals with fewer safeguards catch
   up.

### Impact on product development

- Score the case, never the person: no rankings, anonymous handles, roles instead of names.
- Explainable scores. The AI advises and a human decides.
- Export, deletion, retention and an audit log built in.
- A works-council pack and an AI off switch per company.
- An invention flag and a premium export that follow the works agreement.

## Environmental factors

*Ecological factors, climate change and sustainable practices that affect NextUp's market.*

- **Climate targets in automotive:** EU fleet CO₂ limits and the shift to electric cars push OEMs and suppliers to cut
  emissions in production.
- **Supply-chain pressure:** OEMs ask suppliers for CO₂ data and reductions (Scope 3), and sustainability reporting
  (CSRD) asks for proof.
- **Resource use:** energy, scrap and material waste on the shop floor, the area where employees spot savings first.
- **Green IT:** AI and data centres use energy and water, and buyers ask where and how efficiently software runs.

### Scenarios

1. **Steady:** CO₂ goals stay as they are. Sustainability is a "nice to have" next to money saved.
2. **Green push:** stricter CO₂ and reporting rules mean ideas must prove CO₂ and energy savings, not just money saved.
3. **Green slowdown:** CO₂ goals and reporting are weakened or delayed. Sustainability ideas lose priority against
   pure cost-cutting.

### Impact on product development

- CO₂, energy (kWh) and scrap as standard measures, checked before and after at +30 and +90 days.
- An export of saved CO₂ and energy for the customer's sustainability report.
- A "sustainability idea" type, so green ideas can be found and run as campaigns.
- Low-footprint AI: an AI-off option, small models where they suffice, and EU hosting in efficient data centres.

The legal points come from the research in this folder; the environmental points are a synthesis without their own
research and should be sourced before external use.
