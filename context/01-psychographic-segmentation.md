# NextUp hears employees; trust still gates buyers

As of 2 Nov 2026, NextUp's design fits the employee-facing segments well, but it is not yet a product a German automotive buyer or works council can sign off. The strongest fits are **Desire to be heard** (a 5-day answer promise with deputy fallback), **Evidence Based Decision Making** (fact-cited, arithmetic scores and a before/after measure "computed from the numbers, never typed") and **Low tolerance for extra work** (one chat, one question per turn, four leader buttons). These match the strongest external evidence: employees quit idea schemes because of silence and opacity rather than small bonuses, and typical German decisions take about 2.5 months against a best-in-class 14 days. The weakest fits are the segments that procurement, IT and the works council judge: **Trust/Fairness & Data Privacy** and **Need of Control & Accountability**. Every signed-in browser still receives the whole case log, there is no export, deletion or admin audit log, a person ranking survives in the code, and the works-agreement pack does not exist yet. **Recognition & Meaningful Impact** is the biggest asset that is built but hidden: measure and reward work only on demo companies. Three caveats apply. The 5-day clock does not yet fire on a real company. **NextUp has no real users**, so every fit rating below rests on code and design, not behaviour. Most public automotive figures date from 2011–2019. The priority order follows from this: finish CLK, then make VIS, GDP and SEC pilot-gating, switch on LOOP with a minimal reward export, and only then widen reach with NTF, FLR and Teams.

## NextUp's loop is built for voice, but much of it is still demo-only

The product's core loop is raise → route → inbox with a 5-day clock → answer. After a decision comes discuss → project (Kanban) → measure at +30/+90 days → recognise. The idea studio scores ideas on four 0–100 bars that are "arithmetic over the author's own words and the company context, **never model output**". The coach asks **one** challenging question aimed at the weakest bar, and the decider gets a rail of five facts: a number on the saving, who can decide, a rough cost, the company goal and a small first step ([IDEAS.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/IDEAS.md)). The governing principles are "AI proposes, never decides", "Roles, not people" and "Score the case, never the person" ([NextUp – Produkt & Vision](file:///C:/Users/k_sch/Documents/obsidian_vault/03%20Projekte/nextup/nextup%20–%20Produkt%20&%20Vision.md)).

The gap between design and live behaviour is large. The clock, deputy hand-over, check-ins and 30/90-day measures run on `Company.demoDay`, so "on a real company the 5-day promise, deputy hand-over, check-ins and the 30/90-day measures never fall due" (G-001). Discussion, projects, measure and reward are switched on only for demo companies (G-007). Manager dashboard, feed and overview partly run on preview data (G-048 to G-050) ([gaps/product.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/audit/gaps/product.md)). The autonomous PILOT plan (22 phases) finished AUD, ARC, OPS, STR, LNG and CNX on 2 Nov, which added the German UI, structure outside the demo seed, connector interfaces, Jira Data Center and off-box backups. IDN (identity by id) and CLK (real working-day clock) are running. **VIS, GDP, ADM, DIR, NTF, LOOP, MET, TMS, FLR, AIP, API, EID, SEC and TST have not started** ([PILOT.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/projects/nextup/hauptplaene/PILOT.md); [pilot-readiness.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_pilot-readiness.md)). Two decisions are still open: whether the first pilot is a 1,000–2,000-person supplier or a paid OEM PoC ([segment-analysis.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_segment-analysis.md)), and whether the loop flags will be on in that pilot.

## Employees quit idea schemes over silence, not small bonuses

The four employee segments share one story. The latent willingness is large. German OEMs ran tens of thousands of ideas a year: Daimler had **77,000 suggestions with about 44 % implemented in 2011** ([mbpassion](https://mbpassion.de/2013/05/daimler-spart-durch-mitarbeiter-ideen-mehr-als-61-millionen-euro/)), and Dräxlmaier, the most recent automotive data point, had **17,000 ideas with a 59 % implementation rate** when it won "Best Idea Management" 2025 ([Zentrum Ideenmanagement](https://www.zentrum-ideenmanagement.de/ideenmanagement-preistraeger-2025)). Per head, large German firms are weak: **29 % of employees submit, at 0.3 ideas per employee per year against 2.5 in the Mittelstand**, and a decision takes "just under 2.5 months" ([iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)). Toyota reports about **14.4 suggestions per person** (2023) on a model of many small, self-implemented ideas ([Toyota Times](https://toyotatimes.jp/en/series/Imaginative_and_creative/001_1.html)). The Neckel benchmark of 273 firms shows a slow decline, with participation falling almost 2 % a year and implementation more than 7 % ([Neckel 2025](https://www.hartmut-neckel.de/blog/kennzahlenvergleich-ideenmanagement-2025-ergebnisse/)).

**Improvement Oriented Mindset** therefore exists but is suppressed by the "is this big enough?" threshold of the German suggestion-scheme (BVW) form. NextUp fits this well at the raise step: the coach turns a vague complaint into a decidable case, and versions are re-scored during discussion. Follow-through is weaker. The brain does not follow a project after its start, similarity search uses keywords rather than embeddings, and there is no fast quick-win track separate from big business cases ([IDEAS.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/IDEAS.md)).

**Desire to be heard** has the strongest evidence and is the decisive retention driver. In a Böckler-analysed company survey, **44 % said they do not take part because ideas "won't be implemented anyway"** ([Böckler](https://www.boeckler.de/fpdf/HBS-003070/p_bvd_betriebl_verbess_vorschlags.pdf)). Silence research finds that futility and fear of being labelled drive silence, and **74 % of those uncomfortable speaking up said colleagues felt the same** ([Milliken, Morrison & Hewlin 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jomstd/v40y2003i6p1453-1476.html)). Rejection itself is not the problem. Reasoned rejection increases resubmission, and the effect is strongest when the reason uses the submitter's own language ([Piezunka & Dahlander 2019](https://researchgate.net/profile/Linus-Dahlander/publication/326022890_Idea_Rejected_Tie_Formed_Organizations'_Feedback_on_Crowdsourced_Ideas/links/5d7a691a299bf1ec8bd358eb/Idea-Rejected-Tie-Formed-Organizations-Feedback-on-Crowdsourced-Ideas.pdf)). In one suggestion box, rejected submitters kept coming back up to a tipping point of about 27 rejections ([RSM Discovery](https://rsm.nl/discovery/2014/rejection-motivates)). NextUp's design (a 5-day promise, deputy fallback, "No + reason", a mail at every step and the author's `/team` status track) answers this almost point by point. It will not be real until CLK lands, and the reminders, digests and interim messages that keep the promise visible wait for NTF.

**Recognition & Meaningful Impact** splits into two needs. One is a fair material premium, which German employees treat as an entitlement anchored in works agreements: about **€1,400 per implemented idea** on average ([iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)), with VW, Daimler and Bosch paying roughly a fifth to a quarter of first-year savings. The other is seeing the idea live and being credited for it. Field evidence favours reward on acceptance. In an experiment with more than 11,000 employees, it **raised idea quality and broadened participation without crowding out intrinsic motivation** ([Gibbs, Neckermann & Siemroth](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/14045.pdf)). Two warnings apply. Paying a premium for an idea that is never implemented (a "Schweigeprämie", or hush bonus) backfires ([iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)), and at a global automotive company an unusually large reward cut the same person's next-idea implementation by **about 42 %** ([RSM 2025](https://www.rsm.nl/discovery/2025/success-can-stall-innovation/)). Large German ideas often come from pairs or teams ([mbpassion 2014](https://mbpassion.de/2014/03/ideenreichtum-der-mitarbeiter-spart-daimler-748-millionen-euro/)), and team competition is received better than individual leaderboards ([Hammedi et al. 2021](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/handle/2078.5/120007)). NextUp's measure → recognise step, with an anonymous claim and "counts only, no ranking of people", is exactly the reward-on-verified-impact model that competitors lack. It is hidden behind flags, the "reward scheme" is out of pilot scope, and co-authors, team credit, a wall of implemented ideas, a payroll export and an invention flag under the Employee Inventions Act (ArbnErfG) exist only as research recommendations.

**Low tolerance for extra work** is the second-strongest employee lever. "Low-threshold" submission is the first success factor ([iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)), and production workers often have no PC. voestalpine gave them a terminal in the continuous-improvement (KVP) room ([TU Dresden PAL](https://pal.webspace.tu-dresden.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/250813_PAL_Interview-voestalpine.pdf)), a 2025 award winner put a QR code on employee badges ([Zentrum Ideenmanagement](https://www.zentrum-ideenmanagement.de/ideenmanagement-preistraeger-2025)), and Continental takes ideas in 22 languages ([iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)). NextUp is strong for office staff ("No form, no category tree, no ticket", Microsoft SSO, German UI shipped) and weak on the shop floor. There is no QR, kiosk or PWA (G-022/G-023), email is the only channel (G-020), every event sends its own mail (G-026), and invite and reset pages are stubs (G-018) ([gaps/product.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/audit/gaps/product.md)). No source measures how many minutes submitters will tolerate. The 2-minute target from the notes is an inference.

## Managers buy evidence and control, and NextUp leads on only one

Buyers and approvers think in finance terms. The Deutscher Ideenmanagement Report 2026 (122 organisations) reports a **median long-term ROI of 240 % and €7,262 first-year net benefit per implemented idea**, and 81 % rate generative AI as highly important for idea management ([produktion.de](https://www.produktion.de/wirtschaft/ideenmanagement-potenzial-von-216-milliarden-euro/2699640)). Lean practice adds a one-page A3 business case pre-agreed with affected parties (nemawashi) before formal approval ([Toyota UK](https://mag.toyota.co.uk/nemawashi)). On **Evidence Based Decision Making** NextUp is genuinely strong: every score point names its fact, advice reads Approve / Pilot / Needs info, "raised before?" surfaces earlier cases, discussion invites affected people before the decision (which is close to nemawashi), and the verdict at +30/+90 days is computed. Its weak spots are a keyword-based benchmark with only **32 eval ideas, 3 of them German** (G-037), and an M365 knowledge source that is off ([gaps/product.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/audit/gaps/product.md)). It also does not report in the dib/ZI KPI language an idea-management lead uses to defend a budget.

**Risk Awareness & Reliability** works on two levels. On the shop floor, a process idea is a controlled change under IATF 16949 clause 8.5.6.1. It can trigger trials, cross-functional sign-off, customer notification and a new PPAP submission ([16949store](https://16949store.com/iatf-16949-standards/iatf-16949-core-tools/what-is-ppap-training/); [Rockwell/Plex checklist](https://plex.rockwellautomation.com/content/dam/plex/documents/pdf/industries/auto-and-tire/checklist-automotive-quality-req.pdf)), which is why line managers resist "good" ideas. In procurement, **TISAX is a contract requirement**, and suppliers without a label may be dropped from requests for quotation ([Omnex 2026](https://blog.omnex.com/2026/01/02/why-tisax-has-become-a-contract-requirement-for-automotive-suppliers/)). Evidence from adjacent automotive software points to selection and rollout taking 18–24 months and to paid PoCs of €50k–200k ([develop3d](https://develop3d.com/?p=28266); [vendor data room](https://oxidos-data-room.notion.site/Clients-Pricing-model-1165b230005180fd9014d6a0b8df230f)). NextUp has a Risk dial, a risks step in the coach, a fast lane bounded by spend authority, and solid operations (one stack per company, off-box backups, a restore timed against the RTO, pinned digests). It has no change-control awareness, no load test at 2,000 accounts, a brain on a shared CPU server rated for about 10 users, no pentest and no VDA ISA or TISAX ([pilot-readiness.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_pilot-readiness.md)).

**Need of Control & Accountability** maps onto the classic BVW chain: submitter → supervisor or central idea manager → expert reviewer (Gutachter) → decision and bonus, with processing time tracked per role as a KPI ([diplomarbeiten24](https://www.diplomarbeiten24.de/document/140188)). NextUp's ownership model is its pitch ("Who owns this decision?"). Only the case holder decides, only managers re-route or move the clock, and events are append-only ([SECURITY.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/SECURITY.md)). The customer cannot yet operate that control itself. The routing table is read-only (G-012 to G-014), flags can only be switched from admin.sellux.ch (G-007), admin actions go "only to console, not to an audit log" (G-041), and company stacks use a shared admin code without 2FA (G-047). For an IT reviewer this segment is weaker than the pitch suggests.

**Need of transparency** pulls in two directions. Idea-management leads want to see who is sitting on which idea and for how long. Line managers accept that visibility upward and as status for the submitter, but resist it when it exposes their personal backlog (inference from the buyer notes). The works council resists per-person counts. NextUp's wait ledger, which files every waiting day under one of four reasons, and its per-case timeline are the right instruments. The ledger is only partly built, the dashboards partly show placeholders, and the routing proposal and its override are not yet visible ([segment-analysis.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_segment-analysis.md)). The deliberate choice not to escalate late steps per person ("for the works council") correctly resolves the tension in favour of case-level transparency.

## The works council judges NextUp on promises it has not yet built

An AI idea platform triggers two enforceable co-determination rights. §87(1) No. 12 of the Works Constitution Act (BetrVG) covers the principles of the suggestion scheme. §87(1) No. 6 covers any system that is **objectively capable** of monitoring behaviour or performance, whatever its intent ([§87 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html); [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/br-mitbestimmung-betriebliches-vorschlagswesen-HI3548248.html)). Approval of complex systems took **12+ months, falling to 6–8 months (3 for simple systems)** once a structured process with a system profile ("Steckbrief") existed. That figure comes from a cement company, not an OEM ([Böckler BV-Portrait 33](https://www.boeckler.de/fpdf/HBS-008714/p_mbf_bvd_portrait_33_2023.pdf)). Model AI agreements require anonymising or pseudonymising data before AI processing ([Bitkom 2026](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)). IG Metall wants co-determination to continue while the system runs, not only at introduction ([IG Metall metall 05/06 2025](https://www.igmetall.de/dam/jcr:8944399f-d48d-44ec-9e44-1549c89db17c/20250422_Bezirk_05_06_2025_200_komplett_d63db860e355e78e11e89ca222efd0bdd450cfd6.f619f32d5e2f990694350e0ba433f64f.pdf)). OEM works councils are absorbed in restructuring, such as the Mercedes savings package covering about 91,000 employees ([Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/unternehmen/industrie/mercedes-einigung-mit-betriebsrat-so-will-der-autobauer-sparen/100111291.html)), which raises suspicion that "efficiency ideas" mean job cuts. Lean practitioners report the same: operators go quiet when kaizen is linked to layoffs ([Lean Blog 2025](https://www.leanblog.org/2025/11/lean-without-layoffs/)).

For **Trust/Fairness & Data Privacy**, NextUp's design is the best-aligned in the market: roles not people, private drafts (BetrVG §87 is cited explicitly), anonymous handles on by default, redaction before any model call, EU inference, a gate on the date of the data processing agreement (DPA), a fail-closed stage policy and one stack per company. The functions that would prove it are missing. **The whole case log reaches every browser** (G-039). A "Most relied-on contributors" ranking and per-person counts survive (G-045). Users created by Microsoft Entra on first sign-in get no anonymous handle, so their name shows (G-046). There is no export, deletion or retention (G-042 to G-044). The coach skips the DPA gate when the brain answers (G-036). The works agreement template, DPIA, TOMs and VDA ISA are still at stage "idea" ([gaps/product.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/audit/gaps/product.md); [pilot-readiness.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_pilot-readiness.md)). The landing pages already promise "export of everything at any time", a routing table editor and a written works-council description. A works council that finds G-045 or G-039 in a review would see a broken promise, which is worse than an honest roadmap.

On the legal side, AI that scores **ideas** and leaves the decision to humans can plausibly claim the AI Act Art. 6(3) exemption. It becomes high-risk under Annex III point 4 once scores feed into per-person evaluation, and those obligations now apply from **2 Dec 2027** after the AI Omnibus ([Sidley 2026](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/)). GDPR explainability under CJEU C-203/22 requires plain-language logic, not formulas ([Bitkom 2026](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)). That makes "score the case, never the person" a legal moat as well as an ethical stance, but only while G-045 is removed.

**Openness to AI & Changes** in Germany combines high use with low trust. **55 % use AI tools at work, but only 32 % trust AI-generated information and only 20 % have had AI training**, leaving Germany second-to-last of 47 countries on AI literacy ([KPMG 2025](https://kpmg.com/de/en/media/press-releases/2025/05/between-everyday-life-and-concern-two-thirds-of-germans-use-ai-but-only-a-few-trust-the-technology.html)). Explicit fear of replacement is a minority view, at 14–15 % ([Hannover Messe / TÜV](https://www.hannovermesse.de/en/news/news-articles/german-employees-not-ecstatic-about-ai)). VW has given Copilot access to more than 330,000 employees ([drweb.de](https://www.drweb.de/volkswagen-microsoft-copilot-beschaeftigte/)), so staff already have a reference point. Research on algorithmic fairness is mixed: human oversight raises perceived fairness but often stays below purely human decisions ([Starke et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12016)). NextUp serves both ends. Curious users get a coach, routing proposals and AI-drafted plans. Skeptics get a core loop that "works with the AI and every integration switched off" and an AI "off" choice per company (HD7, HD8; [pilot-readiness.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_pilot-readiness.md)). What is missing is the plain-language "AI suggested X because Y; a person decided Z" explanation in the UI, provider choice per company (AIP), and a German eval set large enough to trust.

## Competitors split between light capture and heavy suites, leaving trust unclaimed

The field divides into three groups. The first is enterprise suites: HYPE (German, with Daimler, VW, Continental and Mahle on its client list, an AI Coach, AI Triage and an AI Evaluator Summary), ITONICS (Toyota Motor Europe, Škoda, ISO 27001) and Qmarkets (on-prem option). The second is lightweight SaaS: Ideanote, IdeaScale and HYPE Boards (formerly Viima). The third is SAP-embedded BVW tools such as target Idea Management, which lists BorgWarner, Porsche, VW and ZF and pays premiums through SAP payroll ([HYPE clients](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/en/clients); [HYPE AI](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/ai-value-acceleration); [ITONICS automotive](https://www.itonics-innovation.com/automotive-industry); [softguide](https://www.softguide.de/programm/target-idea-management-fuer-den-einsatz-mit-sap)). Suites win on evidence and control but draw learning-curve and setup complaints ([G2](https://www.g2.com/compare/hype-innovation-vs-qmarkets-impact-driven-innovation-software)). Light tools win on effort and voice. Ideanote's Ultimate plan offers SSO, SCIM, audit log, EU residency and self-hosting, but shows no ISO 27001 or TISAX ([Ideanote pricing](https://www.ideanote.io/pricing)). Competitors motivate with points, badges and leaderboards set by admins. None documents a reward tied to measured impact ([ITONICS blog](https://www.itonics-innovation.com/blog/gamification-and-nudging-mechanisms-idea-management-software); [Brightidea support](https://support.brightidea.com/hc/en-us/articles/205841997)).

Four positions are open. No vendor found frames AI as works-council-ready evaluation support with auditable scoring and no profiling of individuals. No vendor bridges 30-second frontline capture to an automatically built A3 business case. No vendor maps ideas to IATF 16949 change control, PFMEA or PPAP triggers. No idea-management vendor surfaced with a TISAX label. NextUp's design aims at the first two, could own the third cheaply, and would need SEC plus a VDA ISA self-assessment to approach the fourth. The incumbent threat is real: HYPE is the most dangerous direct rival for German OEMs, and SAP-embedded tools own the premium process. NextUp should sit in front of the customer's BVW as the capture, decision and measure layer and feed it, rather than try to replace it. Vendor features and logos are vendor claims and may be out of date.

## Conclusion

The segmentation shows a structural mismatch. NextUp's differentiators serve the people who use it (being heard, little effort, impact-linked recognition), while its adoption is decided by people who guard against it (works council, data protection officer, IT, quality). In German automotive the second group moves first and slowly: a complete works-council pack starts a clock of three to eight months or more, and a TISAX-style review checks functions rather than principles. NextUp's privacy-by-design principles are already stricter than any competitor's public positioning, so it does not need a new trust story. It needs to make the existing one verifiable through VIS, GDP and SEC before CLK and LOOP have real users to show off. The same principle that protects NextUp legally (score the case, never the person) is also what makes its transparency and recognition acceptable to a works council. Removing the leftover person ranking matters more than any new feature.

What remains unknown is how these ten segments actually distribute among real employees. No interview or usage data exists, and the external evidence is mostly pre-2020 company press releases and non-automotive approval timelines. The pilot should therefore be instrumented as segmentation research: days to first reasoned answer, the share of cases raised outside office channels, the AI override rate, and repeat-submission rates after a "No" would show which segments drive participation in DACH automotive. A plant-versus-plant comparison of claim menus agreed with the works council would also give NextUp the first industry evidence on recognition and its strongest sales argument.
