# NextUp sells the answer, still misses BVW plumbing

At the end of its PILOT plan, NextUp's **basic offer** is one job done reliably: an employee's idea becomes a decided, owned case with a reason, and a "yes" becomes a project with a number to beat. Its **augmented offer** is a set of commitments no incumbent publishes: an answer within 5 working days or the deputy takes over, scores that are arithmetic and never model output, impact measured at +30/+90 days before anyone is recognised, a roles-not-people design a works council can sign, and one stack per company, hosted or on-prem. The problem sits one level lower. German suggestion-scheme (BVW) tenders and selection checklists take for granted things NextUp will still lack after PILOT: **premium calculation with payroll hand-off, a formal expert-reviewer (Gutachter) and committee workflow, Zentrum-Ideenmanagement-style KPI reporting, an ISO 27001 certificate and a written support SLA**. The common AI features (duplicate detection, reviewer summaries, translation, a writing coach) no longer count as differentiators, because nearly every vendor now advertises them. NextUp has most of them, but it has no machine translation of idea content. The consequence is direct. In a BVW tender run by an automotive idea-management department, NextUp would fail the expected layer before its differentiators are scored, however good its AI is. The cheapest fix is to put a minimal premium-and-payroll export, a Gutachter step and ZI KPIs into existing PILOT phases (LOOP, MET, PIL) or one small new phase. Certification and SLA are non-code work that has to start alongside. All NextUp statements describe the planned end-of-PILOT state as of 2 Nov 2026. The product has no real customer yet.

## The basic offer is a decision, not a portal

Kotler's basic offer is the core benefit the customer buys. For NextUp that is not "an idea database". It is the step most schemes fail at: getting a named decision with a reason. The product loop is raise → route → inbox → answer, then discuss → project → measure → recognise. A "yes" archives the idea and opens a project with lead, steps and a baseline. A "no" archives it with its reason, and the author sees each stage (Sent, Read, In discussion, Approved, In progress, Done, Measured, Recognised) with a mail at every step ([NextUp IDEAS.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/IDEAS.md)). PILOT turns this from a demo into something that runs for a real 1,000-2,000-person company. Phase CLK makes deadlines, deputy hand-overs, check-ins and measures fall due on a working-day clock. Phase LOOP must prove the whole loop from raise to project on a non-demo company **with AI, n8n, Jira and M365 switched off** ([PILOT Hauptplan](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/projects/nextup/hauptplaene/PILOT.md)). That last rule (HD7) matters for the offer. The core job does not depend on the AI. The AI improves the job, but it is not what the customer buys.

The core job meets a real need. Benchmarks cited by vendors put the average DACH implementation rate at **44.4%** and the volume at **321 ideas per 1,000 employees per year** ([Qmarkets benchmark summary](https://qmarkets.net/resources/article/continuous-improvement-tools-and-benchmarks/)). A vendor-commissioned survey found that **62%** of frontline workers have suggested improvements but only **38%** feel their ideas always reach decision-makers ([SDCExec/Weavix](https://www.sdcexec.com/sourcing-procurement/manufacturing/news/22958291/weavix-frontline-manufacturing-workers-ready-for-ai-study)). The gap between suggesting and being answered is the basic benefit NextUp sells.

## The expected offer: tenders ask for workflow, premiums and reports, not AI

### What a DACH BVW buyer takes for granted

The expected offer is everything a buyer assumes comes with the core. The German tenders found are older (2017-2018) and anonymised, and automotive OEM tenders run privately, so the list rests on those tenders, on the SoftGuide function catalogue and on vendor checklists. Across them the same requirements recur. A ~5,000-person industrial firm asked for worldwide submission, evaluation with status tracking visible "via dashboard or app", reward and recognition, statistics, and German plus English ([softguide tender 17/788](https://www.softguide.de/ausschreibungen/industrieunternehmen-sucht-software-fuer-das-ideenmanagement)). A consumer-goods producer of similar size added an archive with full-text search, **reminder mails for overdue suggestions**, DE/EN/FR, iOS/Android apps and **premium handling**. It also wanted one-time licence costs itemised separately from support, updates and maintenance ([softguide tender 18/1239](https://www.softguide.de/ausschreibungen/renommiertes-produktions-und-handelsunternehmen-sucht-ideenmanagement)). An ~800-person electro/metal firm wanted a committee decision based on the **Gutachter's assessment** and "calculation of the premium via the software" ([softguide tender ~800](https://www.softguide.de/ausschreibungen/industrieunternehmen-sucht-software-fuer-das-ideenmanagement-ca-800)). SoftGuide's catalogue of typical KVP/BVW functions lists reminder and escalation, **automatic duplicate checking**, deputy rules, scoring forms with automatic calculation, premium letters, user-defined KPIs, roles and rights, SSO and API access ([SoftGuide functions](https://www.softguide.de/software-tipps/funktionen-tipps-kvp-bvw-software)). HYPE's own selection checklist tells buyers to design premium calculation "via formulas, tables" and to send the existing **Betriebsvereinbarung** (works agreement) to vendors ([HYPE checklist](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/hubfs/checkliste.pdf)). In SAP-heavy groups the benchmark is target Idea Management, which uses SAP HR data directly, **transfers rewards straight to SAP payroll** and ships more than 50 preconfigured KPIs with org-chart drill-down ([TIMETOACT target](https://www.timetoact-group.com/en/details/target-idea-management-on-premise)).

The works council frames all of this. It has co-determination over the principles of the suggestion scheme under § 87(1) Nr. 12 BetrVG ([Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/br-mitbestimmung-betriebliches-vorschlagswesen-HI3548248.html)). It also has a say over any software that is merely *capable* of monitoring behaviour or performance under Nr. 6, with no de-minimis threshold ([Luther](https://www.luther-lawfirm.com/en/newsroom/blog/detail/dauerbrenner-software-vs-mitbestimmung-87-abs-1-nr-6-betrvg)). A buyer therefore expects configurability to the works agreement and documentation for the council. Those are table stakes, not extras. The same holds for services: onboarding, ongoing training and access to domain experts ([HYPE checklist](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/hubfs/checkliste.pdf)), **ISO 27001** as the security baseline (ITONICS has held it since 2017 ([ITONICS Trust](https://www.itonics-innovation.com/trust))), EU or on-prem hosting, SSO, and data deletion at contract end.

### Common AI features moved from augmented to expected

Since 2024 the AI features that once set vendors apart have spread to nearly all of them. HYPE ships an AI Coach, an AI Evaluator Summary that condenses an idea, its attachments and the discussion for reviewers, and Similarity Search for duplicates ([HYPE AI](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/ai-value-acceleration)). Ideanote detects duplicates across languages, matching a French idea to a Japanese one ([Ideanote](https://ideanote.io/feature/ai-duplicate-detection)). IdeaScale, Ideawake and Aha! (since Nov 2024) all detect duplicates ([IdeaScale](https://help.ideascale.com/idea-duplicate-detection-tool); [Ideawake](https://ideawake.com/product/artificial-intelligence/); [Aha!](https://www.aha.io/blog/just-launched-identify-duplicate-customer-ideas-with-ai)). Crowdworx translates ideas, comments, evaluations and decisions automatically ([Crowdworx](https://www.crowdworx.com/resources/the-most-comprehensive-idea-management-platforms-for-2025)). Even the SAP-based target has similarity search. rready says plainly that AI is becoming a baseline ([rready](https://www.rready.com/blog/top-features-of-idea-management-software)). One vendor-written 2026 guide still calls duplicate detection "a meaningful differentiator", but the feature count across vendors says otherwise. The judgement rests on how widely vendors offer these features, not on tender texts: no DACH tender found explicitly requires AI.

For NextUp this changes the pitch. Its coach (one question at a time, aimed at the weakest bar), its "Already raised" action with similar ideas, and its five-point decision rail for the decider ([NextUp IDEAS.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/IDEAS.md)) all meet the expected layer. They do not lift NextUp above it. Two qualifications apply. Similarity still works on **shared words**, with embeddings parked as "not in this step", so cross-language duplicate matching across Czech, Hungarian or Polish plants is weaker than Ideanote's. And PILOT translates the interface into German and English but not idea content, so **machine translation of ideas and comments is a gap**.

## The augmented offer is a set of commitments rivals have not made

What lifts NextUp above the expected layer is not a feature list. It is a set of promises, each answering a veto holder. **The 5-day answer promise with a deputy** speaks to the employee. HYPE and Qmarkets track configurable deadlines, and rready routes to ~7,000 org units at BMW, but none publishes an answer-time promise to the person who raised the idea ([rready BMW case study](https://rready.com/case-studies/bmw-ideamanagement); [HYPE BVW](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/solutions/employee-suggestion-bvw)). **Scores that are never model output** speak to the works council and the Gutachter. NextUp's four bars are arithmetic over the author's own words and the company context, every point names its fact, and chatting cannot talk the score up ([NextUp IDEAS.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/IDEAS.md)). The rivals move the other way: Qmarkets lets AI fill feasibility, novelty and risk fields ([Qmarkets next-gen AI](https://qmarkets.net/resources/news/introducing-next-gen-ai-innovation-management/)), and HYPE's 2026 relaunch promises AI that will "project the business outcomes of innovations" ([HYPE relaunch](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-innovation-announces-strategic-relaunch)). **Impact measured at +30/+90 days before recognition**, with the verdict computed from the numbers, speaks to the controller. No rival ties a reward to impact measured after implementation. **Roles, not people** (no per-person counts or rankings; MET removes the last ranking) speaks to § 87(1) Nr. 6, under which merely being able to monitor performance triggers co-determination ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/deu/legal-updates/it-mitbestimmung-und-ki)).

Two more items speak to IT. **One stack per company, hosted on a German server or on-prem**, with the AI provider chosen per company and "off" as a supported setting, goes beyond the "EU region" that most SaaS rivals offer ([PILOT Hauptplan](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/projects/nextup/hauptplaene/PILOT.md)). Plain on-prem is closer to expected: HYPE, Qmarkets and target all offer it. **Analysis grounded in company data** means Microsoft 365 search that answers with source permissions (CNX), and the dials show what was checked across the company. This is still differentiating, but the edge is narrowing, because HYPE already claims no hidden training on customer data and supports MCP ([HYPE AI](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/ai-value-acceleration)). Speed of setup belongs here too. Reviewers rate HYPE's setup as its weakest point ([Capterra HYPE](https://www.capterra.com/p/227003/HYPE-Innovation/reviews/)), and G2 reviewers put Qmarkets' implementation at about four months ([G2 Qmarkets](https://www.g2.com/products/qmarkets-innovation-management/pricing)), so a pilot bootstrapped from configuration in weeks (PIL) is a benefit buyers do not normally get.

These differentiators have a weakness. They are commitments, so any incumbent could configure them within one or two release cycles. Today they are also unproven at any customer. An augmented offer only counts once the expected offer gets the vendor past the first round, and that order shapes the gap list below.

## The potential offer: AI that does the paperwork after the yes

The potential offer is what NextUp could add over the next one to three years. The strongest candidates build on what NextUp already does well: decisions with reasons, and measured outcomes.

**Agents that draft the implementation artefact after a yes** (an 8D or A3, an ECR request, a Jira epic with tickets, each approved by a human) extend the brain's existing step and metric proposals. The trend is clear. Gartner expects **40%** of enterprise apps to have task-specific AI agents by 2026, up from under 5% in 2025 ([Gartner](https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-08-26-gartner-predicts-40-percent-of-enterprise-apps-will-feature-task-specific-ai-agents-by-2026-up-from-less-than-5-percent-in-2025)). Researchers have built LLM-plus-RAG 8D prototypes against IATF 16949 practice ([IEOM 2026](https://ieomsociety.org/proceedings/milwaukee2026/191.pdf); [OST thesis](https://eprints.ost.ch/id/eprint/1324/)). The evidence is still early: a 2026 review found only 6 of 47 papers directly on AI in 8D ([IIETA](https://www.iieta.org/node/24363)). Closely related is a **quality and PLM change-control bridge**: inbound 8D, ECR and deviation cases from SAP QM, Teamcenter or Windchill, and outbound decisions back into them. Siemens' 2026 copilots for Teamcenter, Polarion and Opcenter, and its planned third-party agent marketplace, give that bridge a possible partner route ([CIMdata](https://www.cimdata.com/de/industry-summary-articles/item/29194-siemens-unveils-technologies-to-accelerate-the-industrial-ai-revolution-at-ces-2026)).

**An AI Act and works-council compliance pack as a product** is the cheapest candidate. PILOT's SEC phase writes a works-agreement template and a DPIA. The potential offer goes further: ready Art. 26(7) information templates, a model card, a per-decision explanation and an audit export. The high-risk Annex III deadline moves to **2 Dec 2027** under the Digital Omnibus ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement)), but deployers must still inform worker representatives before using a high-risk system at work ([AI Act Art. 26](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/26/)). Because NextUp's scoring is already arithmetic, the pack would document what the product already does rather than retrofit explainability. Whether NextUp's routing even counts as high-risk has not been legally assessed. **An employee-invention (ArbnErfG) workflow** is similarly cheap. An invention flag would produce a separate disclosure in text form, a prompt receipt and a visible four-month clock, after which an unreleased invention is deemed claimed ([Kanzlei Herfurtner](https://kanzlei-herfurtner.de/arbeitnehmererfindung/)). The disclosure must be its own document rather than buried in a chat, which is exactly where a coach-based tool risks burying it.

Three candidates depend on scale or partners. **Cross-company benchmarking** of time to first answer, share answered on time and measured savings per idea would answer leadership's "are we good?" question. The 2026 Deutscher Ideenmanagement Report shows the appetite: a long-run median ROI of **240%**, an average of **€7,262** first-year net benefit per implemented idea, and **81%** rating GenAI's future importance high ([Produktion](https://www.produktion.de/wirtschaft/ideenmanagement-potenzial-von-216-milliarden-euro/2699640)). Such a service needs at least five customers and data-sharing consent. **Voice and no-phone shop-floor capture** fits a floor where **64%** of frontline workers face smartphone restrictions ([SDCExec/Weavix](https://www.sdcexec.com/sourcing-procurement/manufacturing/news/22958291/weavix-frontline-manufacturing-workers-ready-for-ai-study)). **Supplier guest access**, letting named suppliers raise cases into an OEM's NextUp, targets savings at supplier interfaces. Catena-X reached 1,069 data-sharing participants by Sep 2026 ([Automotive Logistics](https://www.automotivelogistics.media/supply-chain/more-thannbsp1000-oems-and-suppliers-now-exchanging-data-through-catena-x-network/2746686)), but no source shows idea sharing on it, so a NextUp-native guest role should come before any Catena-X connector. NextUp's own backlog adds a decision bank ("was this already decided?"), an assistant that follows decided projects, and embeddings-based similarity ([NextUp IDEAS.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/IDEAS.md)). Effort for all of these is an estimate, not a sourced figure.

## Gaps in the expected offer and where to close them

The gaps fall into two kinds: product gaps that fit existing PILOT phases, and non-code gaps that have to run in parallel. The reward scheme is formally outside PILOT, but premium calculation is what a BVW buyer asks for first, so a minimal version should move forward ([PILOT Hauptplan](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/projects/nextup/hauptplaene/PILOT.md)).

| Gap | Why it is expected | Recommended home |
|---|---|---|
| **Premium calculation and payroll export** | Tenders ask for "calculation of the premium via the software"; target pays straight into SAP payroll | New small phase **REW** after LOOP and MET: a works-agreement-configurable formula (savings share plus points table for non-quantifiable ideas), premium letter, CSV export for SAP HCM, SuccessFactors or DATEV. No SAP write adapter |
| **Gutachter and committee workflow** | Committee decides on the expert's assessment; custom scoring forms with automatic calculation | Extend **LOOP**: a reviewer role on the route, an assessment form, a reviewer deadline on the CLK clock with deputy, and a committee decision step. Routes edited in **ADM** |
| **ZI KPI reporting** | Standard DACH KPIs since 2012; target ships 50+ KPIs | Extend **MET**: participation rate, ideas per 100 employees, implementation rate, cycle time, measured savings and premiums, by org unit, minimum group size 5, CSV/Excel export, feeding the PIL pilot report |
| **Machine translation of idea content** | Typical function; multi-plant groups need CZ/HU/PL | **AIP** (per-company provider) or a follow-up to LNG, with the original text always kept |
| **ISO 27001, then TISAX** | Baseline for OEM security reviews; rivals hold it | Non-code, outside orch: start after legal entity and pilot LOI; SEC's VDA ISA self-assessment is the first step |
| **Written support SLA and itemised services** | Tenders split licence from support, updates, maintenance | **PIL**: response times in the support runbook and price sheet. An uptime SLA stays later, because HA and 24/7 are out of scope |
| **Native mobile app** | Tenders asked for iOS/Android apps | Not needed for the pilot: Teams, QR and a PWA cover it. Revisit after the pilot |

Three expected items are covered once PILOT ends and need no new work: reminders and deputies (CLK, NTF), audit, export and deletion (GDP), and enterprise identity (EID, SFX). Two items could not be checked. No current (2024-2026) public tender was found, and whether OEM security reviews demand TISAX for an idea tool, rather than ISO 27001 plus a DPA, is unconfirmed.

## Conclusion

The Kotler lens shows why NextUp's pitch and its tender readiness point in different directions. Everything NextUp is proud of sits in the augmented layer, while every item a German idea-management department checks first sits in the expected layer. There NextUp is thinnest, and the common AI features it has worked on most give it no lift. The fixes are not large in code. Premium export, a reviewer step and ZI KPIs are a few weeks of work on the foundation PILOT already builds. What makes them urgent is the order buyers apply: expected first, augmented second.

The potential layer also says something about positioning. The best candidates (artefact-drafting agents, a compliance pack, the ArbnErfG workflow, benchmarking on measured outcomes) all reuse the same asset: decisions that carry reasons and outcomes that carry numbers. That suggests a path that avoids rebuilding a full SAP-style BVW suite. NextUp can close the expected gaps in their minimal, export-based form, sit in front of the existing payroll and quality systems, and compete on what happens after the yes, where no incumbent has staked a claim.
