# NextUp wins on promises, loses on proof

For a large automotive buyer in DACH, the clearest comparison puts **NextUp next to HYPE Innovation, Qmarkets and rready (BMW SPARK)** on five features: guided capture, a decision owner with an answer deadline, scoring that AI never sets, impact measured before reward, and works-council and hosting readiness. These five are where NextUp's design differs from all three rivals and where OEM buyers, works councils and IT judge a tool. None of the three publishes an answer-time promise to the person who submitted the idea. HYPE and Qmarkets both let AI produce assessments (savings estimates, success forecasts, feasibility and risk fields), and none ties a reward to impact measured after implementation. The honest counterweight is status: NextUp has **no real customer yet**, its 5-day clock fires only on demo companies, the measure-and-reward loop is built but demo-only, and its works-council papers, GDPR functions and certifications are still planned. The rivals hold ISO 27001 (Qmarkets also SOC 2 Type II) and real automotive deployments. The table shows NextUp's position. It is not proof that NextUp is ahead today.

### Caveats that belong under the table

Every competitor cell comes from the vendor's own pages, so read each one as a **vendor claim**; "not shown" and "not found" mean absent from public pages as of 2 Nov 2026, not proven missing. G2 and Capterra returned HTTP 403, so review sentiment comes from search snippets. The rready column describes the BMW SPARK deployment from rready's own case study, and the **25.5% AI-use figure is self-reported** for the first four measured months ([rready BMW case study](https://rready.com/case-studies/bmw-ideamanagement)). HYPE's current automotive logos are Toyota, BMW and Volvo; Daimler, VW, Continental and Mahle no longer appear on its clients page and should not be quoted as HYPE customers ([HYPE clients](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/en/clients)). On the NextUp side, "built" means merged code, not used by a customer: the 5-day clock and deputy hand-over run only on demo companies until phase CLK lands, the measure-and-reward loop sits behind flags that are on for demo companies only, and a "Most relied-on contributors" ranking still exists in code and is due for removal in phase GDP, which contradicts the no-ranking rule until then ([NextUp gaps](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/audit/gaps/product.md)). The reward scheme itself is out of pilot scope ([pilot readiness](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_pilot-readiness.md)).

## Three rivals cover the three ways an OEM could buy instead

The pick follows the default and the notes support it. **HYPE** is the incumbent German suite: Bonn-based, PE-backed, about €30M revenue after buying Planbox, ISO 27001 for six years, with a BVW module and Toyota, BMW and Volvo logos under automotive ([HYPE press release](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-innovation-acquires-planbox-bolstering-leadership-in-strategic-innovation-management); [HYPE ISO 2025](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-renews-iso-27001-certification-for-2025)). **Qmarkets** is the closest feature-for-feature rival, because it covers employee ideas (Q-ideate) and continuous improvement (Q-optimize) and has the strongest compliance sheet, with Hyundai Motor America and a Volkswagen North America logo ([Qmarkets customers](https://qmarkets.net/customer-success/our-customers/); [Qmarkets security](https://qmarkets.net/products/security/)). **rready** is the only vendor with a current, documented, large-scale automotive BVW deployment with AI: BMW SPARK, 130k+ employees, routing across ~7,000 org units ([rready BMW case study](https://rready.com/case-studies/bmw-ideamanagement)). It also competes on the same claims as NextUp (AI routing, pre-review, works-agreement fit, EU sovereignty), which makes it the sharpest head-to-head.

Three runner-ups have a place in specific decks. **ITONICS** should replace Qmarkets when the audience is the innovation, strategy or R&D department, or when the target account appears on its logo wall: Daimler, Bosch, ZF, Brose, Dräxlmaier, MAN, Toyota and Škoda ([ITONICS automotive](https://www.itonics-innovation.com/automotive-industry)). Its use cases are foresight, tech scouting and portfolio rather than frontline improvement, so against a BVW buyer it is the weaker comparison. **target Idea Management** (SAP-embedded, TIMETOACT) should come in when the audience is the Ideenmanagement/BVW department of a VW-group company or ZF; its vendor list names Audi, Porsche, Volkswagen, ZF and BorgWarner, and it pays premiums straight into SAP payroll ([softguide](https://www.softguide.de/programm/target-idea-management-fuer-den-einsatz-mit-sap); [TIMETOACT](https://www.timetoact-group.com/en/details/target-idea-management-on-premise)). Show it as a "status quo" column that NextUp feeds, not as a rival it replaces. **Ideanote** fits only a deck aimed at a mid-size supplier pilot that is comparing low-cost self-serve tools; its plans start at $899/month for 100 users and it shows no automotive case ([Ideanote pricing](https://www.ideanote.io/pricing)).

## Why these five rows and not integrations or price

The five rows are where NextUp's design is distinct and where an automotive buyer's veto holders look. Integrations and price were left out on purpose: HYPE lists Teams, M365, Jira, SAML/OIDC, SCIM, Power BI, REST and an MCP server ([HYPE integrations](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/integrations)), and NextUp cannot win a breadth row today. Each kept row reflects a real buying concern. The answer-time row matters because the main reason employees stop submitting is the belief that nothing will happen, and a German industrial tender for a 5,000-person company explicitly asks for reminders on stalled suggestions and mainly smartphone, tablet or monitor input ([softguide tender](https://www.softguide.de/ausschreibungen/industrieunternehmen-sucht-software-fuer-das-ideenmanagement)). The scoring row matters because works councils have co-determination over the principles of the suggestion scheme ([Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/insights/praemien-ideenmanagement-steuer-rechtliche-fallgrube/)), and HYPE's 2026 relaunch pitches AI that will "project the business outcomes of innovations" ([HYPE relaunch](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-innovation-announces-strategic-relaunch)), the opposite of NextUp's rule that scores are "never model output" ([NextUp IDEAS.md](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/IDEAS.md)). The hosting row is where NextUp is closest to parity rather than ahead, and it stays in because IT will ask it first.

## What to respect and what to attack, rival by rival

### HYPE Innovation: broad and German, but heavy to set up

HYPE's strengths are real. It has the broadest AI suite of the three (coach, triage, evaluator summary, similarity search, an MCP server for Copilot Studio, customer-specific LLMs), a German GmbH holding ISO 27001, an on-prem option, and a BVW page that says the platform adapts to "works council agreements" ([HYPE AI](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/ai-value-acceleration); [HYPE BVW](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/solutions/employee-suggestion-bvw)). It also shows large savings cases such as DHL (€234M) and OSRAM ([HYPE clients](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/en/clients)). Attack three points. First, setup and admin burden: reviewers name a learning curve, complex initial setup and "somewhat rigid" configuration, and Ease of Setup (89%) is HYPE's lowest G2 score ([HYPE G2 PR](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/best-idea-management-software-of-spring-2025-hype-innovation-named-g2-leader); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/227003/HYPE-Innovation/reviews/)). Second, HYPE tracks deadlines but promises the submitter nothing. Third, its relaunch moves toward predictive AI and multinational and public-sector flagships (Siemens, Airbus, UN, ESA), away from the plant floor. That makes "AI estimates your savings" a works-council question NextUp can raise.

### Qmarkets: the compliance benchmark, with AI that fills the assessment

Qmarkets has the strongest trust sheet of the group: SOC 2 Type II audited by PwC, ISO 27001, seven hosting regions including Germany and Switzerland, on-prem, SCIM, and anonymisation with defined handling of leavers ([Qmarkets security](https://qmarkets.net/products/security/)). Do not lead with hosting against Qmarkets, because it is at least at parity there. Attack the AI and the loop instead. Its April 2024 release lets admins define AI-generated "Feasibility Assessment", "Novelty Assessment" and "Risk Management" fields, while the explainability claim ("AI results are always transparent") comes with no mechanism ([Qmarkets next-gen AI](https://qmarkets.net/resources/news/introducing-next-gen-ai-innovation-management/)). Q-optimize tracks the ROI of improvements, but no link from that ROI to rewards was found, and the Teams embed is a charged extra ([Q-optimize webinar](https://www.qmarkets.net/?p=1055185); [Microsoft-hosted Qmarkets document](https://query.prod.cms.rt.microsoft.com/cms/api/am/binary/RW1lCvj)). One Capterra reviewer calls it "more content oriented than people oriented" ([Capterra Qmarkets](https://www.capterra.com/p/83531/Qmarkets/reviews)).

### rready: the BMW proof point, with a frontline adoption problem

rready is the rival to respect most in automotive. BMW's Head of Idea Management endorses it, it runs SPARK for 130k+ employees with coaches, a plausibility check, an expert-review assistant, department routing and a "rules and works-agreement assistant", and it offers ISO 27001, Swiss jurisdiction and CH/EU or on-prem hosting ([rready BMW case study](https://rready.com/case-studies/bmw-ideamanagement); [sovara](https://rready.com/sovara); [rready BVW](https://rready.com/bvw)). Attack three things it reports itself. Only **25.5% of contributors** used any AI function, and adoption was "slower than expected, particularly in production environments". Routing goes to a department, not to a named owner with a deadline. "Proactive" assistants are listed as BMW's next stage, which is the guided capture NextUp already ships. rready's platform pitch has also widened: under the sovara brand it sells a general work platform with Jira-style project features and a cost comparison against Atlassian ([sovara](https://rready.com/sovara); [rready pricing](https://www.rready.com/pricing)). That gives NextUp the "we do one thing: get the decision made" line.

## Where NextUp loses today

NextUp loses on proof, certification and finished functions. It has **no customer, no reference and no usage data**: every running company holds made-up data, and the pilot criteria (≥70% answered within 5 days, one case per 10 employees) are proposals ([pilot readiness](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_pilot-readiness.md)). It holds **no ISO 27001, SOC 2 or TISAX label**; a VDA ISA self-assessment, pentest, DPIA, TOMs and the works-agreement template sit in phase SEC, which has not started. The GDPR functions an IT review checks first are missing: no export, no person deletion, no retention, admin actions logged only to the console, and the whole case log reaches every signed-in browser until phase VIS ([NextUp gaps](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/audit/gaps/product.md)). Shop-floor reach is weaker than rready's mobile capture: Teams, QR, kiosk and PWA are all planned, and email is the only channel. The AI "brain" runs on a shared CPU server rated for about ten users. On integrations and payroll, HYPE and target are far ahead, and NextUp has explicitly ruled SAP adapters and the reward scheme out of pilot scope. The landing page also claims "export of everything at any time" and a routing-table editor that the code does not have yet, so the deck should not repeat those claims until phases GDP and ADM ship.

## Conclusion

The table makes a narrow but defensible claim. NextUp is the only option in this set that promises the submitter an answer by a date, keeps AI out of the score, and pays recognition on measured rather than estimated impact. Those are commitments rather than features, which is why the incumbents with more features have not copied them. The risk is that every NextUp cell carries a status qualifier while every rival cell describes something running in production. The table works in front of an OEM only if the "built" cells become "live at pilot company X" before the rivals turn their configurable deadlines into a stated promise. Phases CLK, VIS, GDP and SEC are therefore sales work as much as engineering work. Until they ship, the strongest pitch to a German OEM is to sit in front of the existing SAP-based BVW system and feed it, not to replace it.
