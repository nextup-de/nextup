# NextUp's code is copyable, its trust is not

At the end of the PILOT plan, **nothing NextUp owns sits on the top rung, "Cannot be imitated"**. The code, the integrations and the compliance templates can all be rebuilt, and NextUp's own build speed proves it: its orch system shipped 6 PILOT phases (about 17 person-weeks of classic effort) in roughly 36 hours. The only assets that are hard to copy are the **specification and decision corpus** (24 phase briefs, 14 cross-phase decisions, a 101-row gap register, 8 ADRs, 85 proof-backed tasks) and the **works-council-first, automotive-specific design**. Both can still be re-derived in months. The strong rungs need things PILOT does not deliver, because PILOT ends with **no customer, no signed pilot, no trademark, no certification and no legal entity in scope**: a signed works-agreement annex at a named customer, a first reference with measured before/after outcomes, a registered trademark, and an outcome dataset protected by contract. For each attacker this means the following. An incumbent (HYPE, Qmarkets, rready) already owns the slow layers and needs only **one to two release cycles** to match NextUp's distinctive features. An AI-native startup can match end-of-PILOT NextUp in **about 2-6 months** (estimate), because at that point NextUp has no certification or reference either. A customer's own IT can prototype a narrow version in weeks but needs months of internal approvals to run it. NextUp's lead is therefore a matter of time, not of any asset. It has to turn the next 6-12 months into trust assets that a copier cannot buy.

All copy times and euro figures in this report are **estimates**, built from the research notes. Where a figure has a published source, it is cited.

## The imitability ladder at end of PILOT

| Level | NextUp resources (end of PILOT) | Copy time (estimate) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cannot be imitated** | Nothing yet. Later: registered NextUp trademark, signed works-agreement annex at a named customer, first measured-outcome OEM reference, contract-protected outcome data. | Not reachable in PILOT. Earliest candidates take 1 week (trademark) to 12-24 months (reference) |
| **Difficult to imitate** | Specification and decision corpus (24 briefs, HD1-HD14, 101-row gap register, ADRs); automotive and works-council domain framing; orch speed combined with that know-how. | 2-6 months to re-derive |
| **Can be imitated at a cost** | M365/Entra/Jira/Teams/SAML/SCIM/SFX integrations; per-company and on-prem ops; compliance pack templates; eval sets, prompts, routing and scoring rules; Musterwerk test suite; orch method. | 1-6 months, tens of k€ |
| **Easy to imitate** | UI and workflow (chat coach, inbox, Kanban, dashboards), LLM features, DE/EN catalog, public claims, unregistered name, open networks (CODE, associations). | Days to 4 weeks |

The ladder rests on three facts. First, what PILOT builds: 24 phases from audit to pilot package, with external systems behind eight connector interfaces, one stack per company (hosted or on-prem), roles instead of people, and AI provider "off" as a supported setting ([PILOT Hauptplan](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/projects/nextup/hauptplaene/PILOT.md)). Second, what PILOT explicitly leaves out: legal entity and insurance, a signed DPA, an external pentest, a TISAX assessment, and finding and running the pilot customer ([PILOT Hauptplan](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/projects/nextup/hauptplaene/PILOT.md)). Third, the resource-based view: an advantage lasts only when a resource is path-dependent, causally ambiguous or socially complex ([VRIO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VRIO)). At end of PILOT, none of NextUp's resources is path-dependent in that sense, because none has yet been through a customer.

The phase count needs a short note. The overview plan numbers **22 phases (0-21)** ([pilot readiness plan](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/docs/plans/2026-11-01_pilot-readiness.md)). The orch Hauptplan re-cuts them into **24 orch-sized phases** and added SFX (SAP SuccessFactors org chart) on 1 Nov. This report uses the 24. By 2 Nov, 6 were done (AUD, ARC, OPS, STR, LNG, CNX), 3 were running and 4 had written build plans. At the 1-2 Nov pace, the code side of PILOT plausibly finishes in mid to late November 2026, before the 11 Dec pitch (estimate).

## Thirty-six hours for six phases cuts both ways

The strongest evidence about NextUp's imitability comes from NextUp itself. On 1-2 Nov 2026 orch, Kevin's own build controller (about 6.2k lines, started 30 Oct), ran three parallel Claude lanes. Each task carries a proof command that is fixed before the build and run by orch, never by the session that builds ([plans README](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/plans/README.md)). In about 36 hours it merged the acceptance PRs for CNX (#272), STR (#296), OPS (#298) and LNG (#299). In the overview plan these phases account for roughly **17 classic person-weeks**. That is about **20x the speed of the planned 2.5-FTE team and 50-60x one developer** (estimate; part of CNX and OPS existed before PILOT). The whole product took **7 weeks and about 370 merged PRs** to reach about 116k lines across four repos, with Kevin as author of about 84% of the Nextup commits ([Nextup repo history](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/Nextup/)). At classic speed the replacement cost would be about **90-105 person-weeks** (estimate).

This speed is NextUp's best operating capability and also the proof that its code is a weak moat. Anyone with the same tools can apply the same method. Geoff Huntley's "Ralph" loop has cloned commercial products with Claude Code at roughly **US$10 per compute hour** ([LetsDataScience](https://letsdatascience.com/news/developer-automates-coding-assistants-to-clone-software-ba267e3e)). The early-2026 "SaaSpocalypse" sell-off priced in exactly this threat to software vendors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/01/saas-in-saas-out-heres-whats-driving-the-saaspocalypse/)). European law gives almost no shelter. Under *SAS Institute v WPL*, a program's functionality, language and file formats are not protected expression, and a deliberate functional emulator did not infringe ([Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/sas-institute-inc-v-world-programming-limited)). Software patents require a "further technical effect" that a workflow and routing app rarely has ([EPO G-II 3.6](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2024/g_ii_3_6.html)), and grants take two to four years ([EPO](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guide-epc/2024/ga_c2_4_3.html)).

Two qualifications explain why the code is not worthless as a barrier. Agent speed does not compress everything equally. In METR's RCT, experienced developers were **19% slower** with AI while believing they were 20% faster ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)). AI-generated code introduced vulnerabilities in **about 45%** of Veracode's tasks ([Veracode](https://www.veracode.com/press-release/ai-generated-code-poses-major-security-risks-in-nearly-half-of-all-development-tasks-veracode-research-reveals/)). DORA finds that AI "amplifies" existing strengths and weaknesses rather than levelling teams ([DORA](https://www.dora.dev/research/ai)). Hardening, security and integration therefore stay slow for any copier. The second qualification matters more. orch is fast because it is told exactly what to build. The 85 tasks with proofs, the HD1-HD14 decisions (for example "roles, not people", "SAP read-only, org chart only", "AI off supported") and the gap register record what NextUp learned about automotive buyers, works councils and IT reviews. A copier sees the running product, not why it is shaped that way. That **specification corpus is NextUp's most valuable hidden asset**, and it stays hidden only while NextUp protects it. Under German trade-secret law, anything observable in a product that is made available may be reverse-engineered unless a contract forbids it ([GeschGehG §3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geschgehg/__3.html)).

## Incumbents need one release, startups a season, customer IT a works agreement

The question "how fast could they copy it" splits into four layers, and each attacker type faces them in a different order.

| Layer | (1) Incumbent: HYPE, Qmarkets, rready | (2) New startup with AI coding tools | (3) Customer's own IT |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Functional clone** of NextUp's distinctive loop (answer deadline, deterministic scoring, measured impact before reward, chat coach) | 1-2 quarters, gated by priority, not capacity | 1-4 weeks for the core, plus 1-2 months to re-derive the spec | 2-8 weeks for a narrow internal prototype |
| **Enterprise hardening and integrations** (Entra/SCIM, M365, Jira, Teams, on-prem, GDPR functions) | Already owned | 2-6 months | 2-6 months, easier tenant access but own security review |
| **Compliance and certification** (ISO 27001, TISAX AL2, DPA/TOMs/DPIA, works agreement) | Already owned (ISO 27001; Qmarkets also SOC 2 Type II) | 3-12 months, about €15-60k | No external label needed; works agreement and DPIA 3-12+ months |
| **First reference customer** | Already owned in automotive; needs one pilot to prove the new promises | 12-24 months | Not needed; replaces NextUp at that account |
| **Total to match end-of-PILOT NextUp** | About 1-2 quarters | **About 2-6 months** | About 3-12 months to a sanctioned internal tool |
| **Total to match NextUp after a successful pilot** | Same, and they keep their bundle | 12-24 months | Depends on works-council speed |

All cells are estimates synthesised from the notes. The sources follow.

**Incumbents** are the most dangerous copiers even though they are the slowest coders, because they already own every slow layer. HYPE holds ISO 27001, offers on-prem, and lists Teams, M365, Jira, SAML/OIDC, SCIM and an MCP server ([HYPE integrations](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/integrations); [HYPE ISO 2025](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-renews-iso-27001-certification-for-2025)). Qmarkets has SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and DE/CH hosting ([Qmarkets security](https://qmarkets.net/products/security/)). rready runs BMW SPARK for 130k+ employees with AI routing across about 7,000 org units and a works-agreement assistant ([rready BMW case study](https://rready.com/case-studies/bmw-ideamanagement)). NextUp's differentiators are commitments: a 5-day answer promise to the submitter, scores that are "never model output", and recognition tied to impact measured at +30/+90 days. Any of the three could configure these with existing phase-gates and KPIs. AI fast-follow now runs in weeks: Copilot matched Cursor's agent features with "almost no difference in functionality" by early 2025 ([Kinto](https://blog.kinto-technologies.com/posts/2025-02-25-cursor-vs-copilot)). The historic pattern is that bundling beats the better point product. Teams overtook Slack within about two years largely because it came free inside Office 365 ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1670558/microsoft-teams-now-has-13m-daily-active-users-outpacing-rival-slack.html)). The brake on incumbents is strategic. HYPE's 2026 relaunch moves toward AI that will "project the business outcomes of innovations" ([HYPE relaunch](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-innovation-announces-strategic-relaunch)). That is the opposite of NextUp's "AI never scores" rule, so copying it would mean contradicting their own pitch.

**A new AI-native startup** copies the code layers fastest and the trust layers no faster than NextUp. At the end of PILOT NextUp itself has no label and no reference, so parity with end-of-PILOT NextUp is mostly a matter of rebuilding hardened integrations and re-deriving the spec, about 2-6 months. Parity with a NextUp that has completed a pilot takes far longer. ISO 27001 takes about 3-6 months for a small clean-cloud startup at about €10-25k in year one ([SECJUR](https://www.secjur.com/blog/iso-27001-kosten); [Sprinto](https://sprinto.com/de/hub/iso-27001-timeline/)). TISAX AL2 costs about €15-60k all-in and takes 3-12 months, and about 40% of first assessments end with findings ([SECJUR TISAX](https://www.secjur.com/blog/tisax-kosten)). Enterprise buying cycles run 6-18 months ([Pedowitz](https://www.pedowitzgroup.com/how-long-are-enterprise-software-journeys)), and a reference cannot be bought at all.

**A customer's own IT** is the attacker that contracts must handle, because it skips procurement entirely. It can prototype a narrow internal tool in weeks. Running that tool in production triggers the same co-determination as NextUp: any system objectively able to monitor behaviour or performance needs works-council consent under § 87(1) Nr. 6 BetrVG ([CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/ki-transformation-und-betriebsrat-mitbestimmung-richtig-gestalten)). Documented approval times run from **3 months for simple systems to 12+ months for complex ones** ([Hans-Böckler-Stiftung](https://www.boeckler.de/fpdf/HBS-008714/p_mbf_bvd_portrait_33_2023.pdf)). Commentators warn of "build remorse" and AI debt when firms build in-house ([CIO](https://www.cio.com/article/4189693/how-ai-agents-are-shaping-the-future-of-work.html)). A pilot also hands this attacker NextUp's Docker images and its full workflow. The main protection is therefore the pilot contract, not IP law.

## The trust layer is where NextUp can climb

The layers that resist copying are the ones built with a specific customer over time. Approval of a works agreement is socially complex: the council, HR and the data-protection officer have to trust a specific vendor's description of what is and is not measured. A rival must restart that process at the same account, and large groups use framework agreements with **system-specific annexes** for exactly this reason ([Skill Sprinters](https://skill-sprinters.de/blog/compliance/betriebsrat-ki-mitbestimmung/)). References remain among the strongest buying inputs, and peer validation still closes B2B deals ([MarketScale/TrustRadius](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-and-peer-reviews-still-close-the-deal)). In German idea management the buyers meet in a tight community, for example at Zentrum Ideenmanagement and around the Deutscher Ideenmanagement Preis ([DRÄXLMAIER](https://www.draexlmaier.com/en/news/detail/draexlmaier-wins-the-german-idea-management-award)). Enterprise customers churn far less than SMB customers, and the highest-ACV accounts retain best ([SaaS Capital](https://www.saas-capital.com/research/saas-retention-benchmarks-for-private-b2b-companies/)). Accumulated data counts only once it has accumulated. After a 3-4 month pilot the routing and outcome dataset is small, and the customer can demand export.

NextUp's design gives it a real head start on this layer. The landing page already promises "It measures cases. Never people." ([landing page](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/repos/nextup-landing/src/app/(marketing)/page.tsx)). Phase SEC produces a works-agreement template, DPIA, TOMs, record of processing, DPA template and VDA ISA self-assessment, and phase GDP produces export, deletion and audit. These are templates, not signed agreements, so they sit at "imitable at a cost". The VDA ISA self-assessment is close to "easy", because OEM departments typically require a TISAX label rather than a self-assessment ([Docusnap](https://www.docusnap.com/en/it-documentation/tisax-overview)). The network rung is empty. The outreach log shows 25 contacts and no recorded replies, the pilot company is not yet found, and the team is four students whose project is graded on 11 Dec ([NextUp status note](file:///B:/06_CODE/06_Projects/Startup_speed/research_notes/NextUp%20psychographic%20segmentation/nextup_current_status.md)). A bus factor of about one, with Kevin writing 84% of commits, is a fragility rather than a moat.

## Eight moves that climb the ladder, in order

The moves below are ranked by how much defensibility each buys per euro and per week. Costs without a citation are estimates.

| # | Move | Resource it lifts (from → to) | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Register the trademark** after a clearance search: IGE (CH) and EUIPO, classes 9 and 42; secure a production domain to replace test domain sellux.ch | Brand: easy → cannot (name only) | CHF 450 + €900, SME Fund may refund part | 6 working days (IGE expedited) to about 4 months |
| 2 | **Trade-secret hygiene file**: classify spec corpus, prompts, eval sets and scoring weights; need-to-know repo access; specific (not catch-all) NDAs; written IP assignment among the four founders | Spec corpus: difficult (fragile) → difficult (enforceable) | About €0-3k lawyer review | 1-2 weeks |
| 3 | **Pilot contract clauses**: no reverse engineering, no benchmarking or competing development, licence terms for on-prem images, feedback IP to NextUp, case-study right, rights to anonymised outcome data | Code and data vs customer IT: easy → at a cost | About €2-5k for a template | 2-3 weeks, before the first pilot |
| 4 | **Turn the works-agreement template into a signed annex** at the pilot customer, ideally attached to its existing idea-management agreement | Trust: at a cost → difficult (cannot at that account) | Mostly founder time | 3-12 months |
| 5 | **First reference with measured outcomes**: baseline before go-live, ≥70% answered within 5 days, written case study, presentation in the idea-management community | Reference: none → difficult | Pilot effort; venture-client pilots run about 4 months | 4-12 months |
| 6 | **Certification path**: VDA ISA self-assessment (SEC), then ISO 27001 once a legal entity and a pilot LOI exist, then TISAX AL2 | Compliance: easy → at a cost (a gate, not a moat) | About €10-25k ISO, plus €15-40k TISAX | 3-6 months, then about 3-4 months |
| 7 | **Contract-protected outcome dataset**: anonymised answer-time, wait-ledger and measured-impact benchmarks across customers | Data: easy → difficult over years | Engineering plus contract terms | Grows with each customer |
| 8 | **Second maintainer for orch and the spec**: documented runbooks, a second person able to run lanes | Build capability: fragile → durable | Team time | 1-2 months |

The cheapest move comes first. A trademark is the only asset NextUp can put on the top rung within days. The IGE charges **CHF 450** and can register in about **6 working days** with expedited examination ([law.ch](https://law.ch/lawnews/2020/03/vorgezogene-markenpruefung/)). The EUIPO charges **€850 for one class and €50 for the second**, and the SME Fund may refund part of it ([harlander-partner](https://harlander-partner.eu/en/shop/official-fees-for-the-eu-trademark/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/trademark/1772570/euipo-sme-fund-2026-reduce-trademark-costs-for-smes)). The limit is that a trademark protects the name, not the product. "NextUp" is also a common-sounding word, so a clearance search must come before filing.

Trade-secret hygiene has to be documented to count. In 2024 the Federal Labour Court refused an injunction because the claimant could not prove "angemessene Geheimhaltungsmaßnahmen" (reasonable secrecy measures). It also held catch-all confidentiality clauses invalid ([Haufe on BAG 8 AZR 172/23](https://www.haufe.de/id/entscheidung/bag-urteil-vom-17102024-8-azr-17223-HI16740137.html); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/deu/legal-updates/catch-all-lose-all-eine-neue-gefahr-fuer-geschaeftsgeheimnisse)). For NextUp this means private repos with named access, a short written list of what is secret, and PILOT briefs kept out of pitch material. Because NextUp is a university group project, written IP assignment among the four founders matters before any investor or customer asks who owns the code. That point is an inference and needs legal advice.

The pilot contract is the main lever against attacker (3). Reverse engineering of a product lawfully in the tester's possession is allowed unless contractually restricted ([GeschGehG §3](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geschgehg/__3.html)), and on-prem deployment ships the code to the customer. The same contract should secure the case-study right and the right to anonymised outcome data. Those rights feed moves 5 and 7. The sui generis database right protects substantial investment in a curated dataset, not app features ([Directive 96/9/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1996/9/oj)).

Certification comes after the first pilot LOI, not before, because it is a gate rather than a moat. Suppliers without a TISAX label are reportedly excluded from new RFQs at VW, and software vendors are in scope ([CSN partner wiki](https://csn-partner.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/CSNP/pages/9536380/Information+Security+TISAX)). A funded rival can buy the same label within a year. If an existing ISO 27001 ISMS is in place, TISAX takes about 3-4 months ([SECJUR TISAX](https://www.secjur.com/blog/tisax-kosten)), so ISO first is the efficient order. BSI C5 and SOC 2 Type II take 9-18 months and are rarely asked of an idea-management vendor in DACH automotive. They stay off the list unless a customer asks ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/11/soc-2-type-ii-saas-startups-trust-services-criteria-audit-cost-six-month-observation-window-first-examination-guide)). A venture-client route such as BMW Startup Garage issues a supplier number and a first PO for a roughly 4-month pilot ([BMW Startup Garage](https://www.bmwstartupgarage.com/en/benefits-and-program.html)). BMW, however, already runs rready for idea management, so a mid-size supplier is the more realistic first reference.

A patent is deliberately not on the list. A workflow, scoring and routing app sits close to the business-method exclusion, and a patent family costs five figures and takes years ([EPO G-II 3.6](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2024/g_ii_3_6.html); [iamip](https://iamip.com/understanding-patent-lifetimes-and-costs-in-2025/)). The same money buys most of an ISO 27001 certification.

## Conclusion

The usual worry, "someone will copy our code", is the wrong frame for NextUp. Its own orch run shows that code is now the cheapest layer for anyone. What NextUp actually holds at end of PILOT is a **head start in knowing what to build** for a works-council-sensitive automotive buyer. That head start lasts only as long as it stays secret and only until a rival learns the same lessons from its own pilots. PILOT is therefore best read as buying a 2-6 month window, not a moat. The window has value only if NextUp spends it on the one thing no copier can compress: a signed works-agreement annex and a measured before/after result at a real customer, under a contract that keeps the data and the case study with NextUp.

That changes what "done" should mean for the next phase. Phases SEC, GDP and PIL, together with the non-code work outside PILOT (legal entity, trademark, NDAs, the pilot contract), lift NextUp up the ladder more than any further feature phase. They should be scheduled as top priority, not left as "not orch work". The incumbents' real advantage is that their trust layer exists already. NextUp's real advantage is that its product promises (answer by a date, AI never scores, people never ranked) are the kind a works council can sign. The race is about which of the two arrives first at the next automotive account.
