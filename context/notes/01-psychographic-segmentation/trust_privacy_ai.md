# Trust, Fairness, Data Privacy and AI-Openness: Introducing an AI-Assisted Employee Idea Platform at Large German Automotive Companies

Research date: 2 Nov 2026. Scope: Germany/DACH, OEMs and large Tier-1s. Older material is flagged with its year.

## Works council co-determination (§87(1) Nr. 6 and Nr. 12 BetrVG), typical agreements, approval durations, OEM examples

### Takeaway
An AI idea platform triggers at least two enforceable co-determination rights: Nr. 12 for the principles of the suggestion scheme (procedure, evaluation and reward rules, but not the individual bonus amount) and Nr. 6 because any system that is *objectively capable* of capturing behaviour or performance needs consent, whatever the intent. Approval of a complex IT system has taken 12+ months in documented German cases. With an established process it falls to about 6-8 months, or about 3 months for simple systems. No AI-specific group works agreement at VW, BMW or Mercedes could be confirmed from public sources. Mercedes was still negotiating one in 2024.

### Cited Findings
- §87(1) Nr. 6 BetrVG covers the introduction and use of technical equipment "dazu bestimmt" (intended) to monitor behaviour or performance. Case law reads this as objective suitability, with no monitoring intent required. — [gesetze-im-internet.de §87 BetrVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/betrvg/__87.html); [drweb.de, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.drweb.de/volkswagen-microsoft-copilot-beschaeftigte/); [KI-Syndikat (Sep 2026)](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/fuer-unternehmen/betriebsvereinbarung-ki/)
- §87(1) Nr. 12 BetrVG: the works council co-determines only the *Grundsätze* (abstract-general rules on how suggestions are handled). Setting the individual bonus is not subject to co-determination. Cited BAG decisions: 28.4.1981 (1 ABR 53/79) and 16.3.1982 (1 ABR 63/80). These were taken from secondary sources and not checked in full text. — [betriebsrat.de lexicon](https://www.betriebsrat.de/betriebsratslexikon/br/betriebliches-vorschlagswesen); [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/id/beitrag/br-mitbestimmung-betriebliches-vorschlagswesen-HI3548248.html)
- Bonus rules can also fall under Nr. 10 (pay structure), and idea tools that touch working time can fall under Nr. 2. Inventions are governed by the Arbeitnehmererfindungsgesetz, so no co-determination applies to them. — [WEKA](https://www.weka.de/betriebsrat-personalrat/ideenmanagement-so-bestimmt-der-betriebsrat-mit/)
- BAG 19.5.2015 (9 AZR 863/13), on a suggestion bonus under a works agreement: whether a bonus is owed depends on whether the idea lies inside the submitter's own job scope. In that agreement, the bonus for measurable benefit was 25% of net annual savings. This is an example of the kind of rules that typically sit in an Ideenmanagement BV. — [anwalt24 / BAG 9 AZR 863/13](https://www.anwalt24.de/urteile/bag/2015-05-19/9-azr-863_13)
- Other participation rights relevant to AI, per Bitkom's guide "KI und Mitbestimmung" (Feb 2026): information and consultation in good time under §90(1) Nr. 3 and §90(2), with documents provided (§80(2)); an external expert is always deemed "necessary" for AI questions (§80(3) S.2); §95 for selection guidelines. A framework AI agreement (KI-Rahmen-BV) is voluntary and cannot be forced. Companies choose between case-by-case approval, an addendum to the existing IT framework BV, or a separate AI framework BV. — [Bitkom Leitfaden KI und Mitbestimmung, Feb 2026](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)
- Typical AI framework BV contents recommended in model texts: anonymise or pseudonymise personal data before AI processing, joint AI committees, and a "Einigungszwang" (agreement required) clause before AI is used in HR. — [Bitkom guide](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf); [Däubler model Rahmen-KBV KI (Nov 2025)](https://www.daeubler.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Nach-0653.Rahmenkonzernbetriebsvereinbarung-KI1.pdf); [vbw Muster-BV KI, Jul 2025](https://www.bds-bayern.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Muster-Betriebsvereinbarung-zur-Nutzung-von-KI-Juli-2025-vbw.pdf); older real example: [IBM KBR Rahmen-BV KI 2020, commented by ver.di](https://ver-und-entsorgung.verdi.de/++file++655dfad8fab3cf945c65fb45/download/05%20DBRT-RahmenBV%20KI%20kommentiert%20V1.1.pdf)
- **Approval durations (hard data point, 2023):** At Heidelberg Materials, approval times under §87(1) Nr. 6 fell from at least 12 months for complex systems (e.g. Microsoft 365) to 6-8 months maximum, and to 3 months for simpler IT systems, once a structured process with a system profile ("Steckbrief") and a committee was in place. A comprehensive system could need up to 5 negotiation and voting meetings per month. — [Hans-Böckler-Stiftung, BV-Portrait Nr. 33, Oct 2023](https://www.boeckler.de/fpdf/HBS-008714/p_mbf_bvd_portrait_33_2023.pdf) (cement company, not automotive)
- Law firms describe BV negotiations as "regularly" lasting months and delaying new technology. Data protection is frequently the point of contention. Projects slip further when the works council is informed late. — [Görg, 5 Nov 2025](https://www.goerg.de/de/aktuelles/veroeffentlichungen/05-11-2025/keine-erzwingbare-mitbestimmung-beim-datenschutz); [CIO.de](https://www.cio.de/article/3656912/achtung-betriebsrat.html)
- The works council can block a system until a BV exists. If talks fail, the Einigungsstelle (conciliation board) decides. — [CMS](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/ki-transformation-und-betriebsrat-mitbestimmung-richtig-gestalten); [Kliemt blog](https://kliemt.blog/2020/02/25/der-betriebsrat-blockiert-was-tun/)
- LAG Hessen, 5.12.2024, 5 TaBV 4/24 (HR system "People Engine"): there is no *enforceable* co-determination on data-protection design. The GDPR is exhaustive, and data-protection clauses in a BV are voluntary (§88 BetrVG, Art. 88 GDPR / §26(4) BDSG). Co-determination over monitoring under Nr. 6 is unaffected. Görg reads this as allowing leaner, faster BVs. — [Görg](https://www.goerg.de/de/aktuelles/veroeffentlichungen/05-11-2025/keine-erzwingbare-mitbestimmung-beim-datenschutz); [beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/heute-im-recht/rechtsprechung/lag-hessen-5tabv424-datenschutz-unternehmen-betriebsrat-mitbestimmung-2025-07-02)
- ArbG Hamburg, 16.1.2024, 24 BVGa 1/24 (interim proceedings): no co-determination where employees used ChatGPT in a browser with private accounts that the employer could not access. Commentators warn against generalising the ruling. Enterprise tools whose usage the employer can evaluate, such as Copilot with logging visible to admins via Purview, are generally treated as subject to co-determination. — [Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/arbeitsgericht-hamburg-zu-chatgpt-kein-mitbestimmungsrecht-des-betriebsrats); [drweb.de, Oct 2026](https://www.drweb.de/volkswagen-microsoft-copilot-beschaeftigte/)
- **VW:** More than 330,000 employees, about half the workforce, have Microsoft Copilot access, more than 300,000 use Copilot Chat monthly, and more than 26,000 AI agents were active in the prior month (Microsoft release, 21 Oct 2026). The article does not report whether a works agreement exists. — [drweb.de](https://www.drweb.de/volkswagen-microsoft-copilot-beschaeftigte/)
- **Mercedes-Benz:** the GBR activity report 2024 states that a new group works agreement (KBV) on employee data and the use of AI was being negotiated. The 2025 report mentions rising AI applications and digital principles adopted by the works council. Search results also report about 60% of the workforce using AI daily (secondary source). Whether the AI KBV was concluded could not be verified, because the PDF returned 403. — [Mercedes GBR Tätigkeitsbericht 2024](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/dokumente/unternehmen/sonstiges/mercedes-benz-group-gbr-taetigkeitsbericht-2024.pdf); [GBR Tätigkeitsbericht 2025](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/dokumente/unternehmen/sonstiges/mercedes-benz-group-gbr-taetigkeitsbericht-2025.pdf)
- **IG Metall position:** IG Metall's metall magazine (Bezirk edition 5/6 2025) calls for a *process-oriented* co-determination right for AI, so that co-determination continues during operation and is not limited to introduction. An IG Metall works-council survey (2/2025) cited by IG Metall's Detlef Gerst puts AI use at 27% of workplaces. — [IG Metall metall Bezirk 05/06 2025](https://www.igmetall.de/dam/jcr:8944399f-d48d-44ec-9e44-1549c89db17c/20250422_Bezirk_05_06_2025_200_komplett_d63db860e355e78e11e89ca222efd0bdd450cfd6.f619f32d5e2f990694350e0ba433f64f.pdf); [Gerst slides, Arbeitnehmerkammer Bremen](https://www.arbeitnehmerkammer.de/fileadmin/Veranstaltungen/Veranstaltungsarchiv/VAdoku_KI_und_Macht_Detlef_Gerst.pdf); [IG Metall KI page](https://www.igmetall.de/aktive/mitbestimmen/zukunft-der-arbeit-gestalten/kuenstliche-intelligenz)
- Context: OEM works councils in 2026 are occupied with restructuring, for example the Mercedes GBR cost-saving package covering about 91,000 employees and a BMW personnel restructuring agreement of 30 Jul 2026. — [Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/unternehmen/industrie/mercedes-einigung-mit-betriebsrat-so-will-der-autobauer-sparen/100111291.html); [KSW legal (secondary)](https://www.ksw-legal.de/ratgeber/bmw-abfindungsprogramm-2026)

### Inferences
- An AI idea platform at an OEM almost certainly needs (a) a new or amended Ideenmanagement BV for Nr. 12, covering evaluation procedure, AI role, appeal and bonus rules, and (b) clearance under Nr. 6, either through the existing IT/AI framework BV process or a dedicated BV. A realistic planning horizon at an OEM is about 3-8 months after a complete system profile is submitted. Longer is possible if the platform touches HR data or if the works council is busy with restructuring.
- The fastest path is to slot into the customer's existing IT/AI framework BV process: provide a ready "Steckbrief", a data-flow diagram, a role and permission matrix, an explicit list of what the system does *not* do (no individual performance analytics, no ranking of people), and a configurable "works council mode" for exports and reports.
- The job-loss climate created by OEM restructuring makes it more likely that works councils will scrutinise any tool that measures employee contribution or that could surface productivity ideas ("efficiency ideas = job cuts").
- Existing Ideenmanagement BVs typically already define commissions (often parity-staffed), assessors, deadlines and bonus formulas. A vendor should map its workflow onto these rather than replace them.

### Gaps
- No public text of an AI-specific KBV at VW, BMW, Mercedes, Bosch, Continental or ZF was found. The Mercedes reports could not be fetched (403).
- No automotive-specific data on how long works-council approval takes. The 12 → 6-8 → 3 months figures come from one non-automotive company (2023).
- No published IG Metall member survey focused on AI (2025/2026) was found. The 27% figure comes from a slide deck.
- No current sample Ideenmanagement BV from an OEM was found.

## GDPR/DSGVO and §26 BDSG, submitter anonymity, EU AI Act classification and dates (incl. omnibus)

### Takeaway
After CJEU C-34/21 (2023), §26(1) BDSG is shaky as a legal basis. Processing of employee data in an idea platform should rest on GDPR Art. 6 bases plus a BV that meets Art. 88(2) GDPR (CJEU C-65/23, Dec 2024). A BV cannot legitimise processing that is otherwise unlawful. Under the AI Act, AI scoring of *ideas* is not automatically high-risk. It becomes Annex III point 4 high-risk if it evaluates people's performance or behaviour, or influences decisions on promotion or terms. Those obligations were postponed by the AI Omnibus to 2 Dec 2027. Bans (e.g. emotion recognition at work) have applied since Feb 2025.

### Cited Findings
- CJEU C-34/21 (30 Mar 2023): a national rule that merely repeats the GDPR's general conditions is not a "more specific rule" under Art. 88(1). The Hessian provision was identical to §26(1) S.1 BDSG. Commentators split on whether §26(1) S.1 is still usable: some say it can no longer be applied, while BAG 9.5.2023 (1 ABR 14/22) still applied it in combination with a BV. §26(4) BDSG (processing based on collective agreements) is considered safer. — [otto-schmidt Arbeitsrecht-Blog](https://www.otto-schmidt.de/blog/arbeitsrecht-blog/eugh-kippt-beschaftigtendatenschutzrechtliche-generalklausel-ARBLOG0007899.html); [Hugo Sinzheimer Institut slides 2025](https://www.hugo-sinzheimer-institut.de/data/downloads/OEA/Veranstaltungen/2025/v_2025_04_30_schmidt.pdf)
- CJEU C-65/23 (19 Dec 2024, a BAG referral on the cloud HR system Workday): works agreements can be Art. 88 rules but must also satisfy Art. 88(2), i.e. suitable and specific measures for dignity, legitimate interests and fundamental rights. Courts can fully review them, and a BV cannot make otherwise unlawful processing lawful. — [SKW Schwarz](https://www.skwschwarz.de/en/news/cjeu-ruling-on-works-agreements); [Härting](https://haerting.de/en/insights/eugh-strengere-anforderungen-an-betriebsvereinbarungen-mit-datenschutzbezug/)
- No separate employee data protection act (Beschäftigtendatengesetz) has been enacted. A draft (BeschDG) was debated in 2024/25, and practitioners describe a transitional period. — [Uni Saarland slides, Jan 2025](https://www.uni-saarland.de/fileadmin/upload/lehrstuhl/sorge/Vorlesungen/WS_2024-25/250128_PPT_Entwurf_BeschDG_Top_oder_Flop.pdf) (status as of late 2026 not verified)
- Explainability under GDPR: for automated decisions (Art. 22), CJEU C-203/22 (27 Feb 2025) requires precise, understandable information on logic and effects. Source code or formulas are not enough. Bitkom concludes that if no comprehensible explanation is possible, data-protection-compliant use is not possible. — [Bitkom guide, Feb 2026](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)
- Anonymity: model BVs and Bitkom recommend anonymising (preferably) or pseudonymising personal data before AI processing. — [Bitkom guide](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)
- AI Act Annex III point 4 (employment): AI used for recruitment and selection; for decisions affecting terms, promotion or termination; for task allocation based on behaviour or personal traits; or for monitoring and evaluating performance and behaviour is high-risk (Art. 6(2)). Art. 6(3) exempts systems performing a "narrow procedural task" or not materially influencing decision outcomes. — [Bitkom guide](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf); [EUR-Lex AI Act 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Deployer duties for high-risk systems include human oversight by trained staff, use according to the instructions, relevant input data, and logging. Art. 26(7): employers must inform workers' representatives and affected workers *before* putting a high-risk system into use at the workplace. Affected persons gain a right to an explanation of individual decisions (Art. 86). Emotion recognition at the workplace is prohibited (Art. 5(1)(f)) except for medical or safety reasons. — [Bitkom guide](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)
- Art. 4 AI literacy has applied since Feb 2025. — [drweb.de](https://www.drweb.de/volkswagen-microsoft-copilot-beschaeftigte/). The AI Omnibus shifts it to an obligation on the Commission and Member States to "support and facilitate" literacy, rather than a direct duty on deployers. — [Sidley Data Matters, 22 Jun 2026](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/)
- **AI Omnibus timeline:** Commission proposal Nov 2025, provisional trilogue agreement 7 May 2026, European Parliament adoption 16 Jun 2026, Council approval expected afterwards, entry into force 3 days after OJ publication, intended before 2 Aug 2026. Annex III (stand-alone, including employment) high-risk obligations move from 2 Aug 2026 to **2 Dec 2027**, and Annex I (product-embedded) to **2 Aug 2028**. Dates are now fixed, no longer linked to standards. Art. 50(2) marking of AI-generated content moves to 2 Dec 2026 for systems already on the market. — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules). A search snippet reports final Council approval on 29 Jun 2026; this was not verified against the Official Journal.
- Grandfathering: high-risk systems placed in service before the application date stay covered as long as they are not significantly changed (Art. 111(2)). — [Bitkom guide](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)

### Inferences
- An idea platform whose AI scores the *idea* (feasibility, savings potential, duplicates) and leaves decisions to human assessors can plausibly argue it is not high-risk (Art. 6(3)). It loses that argument if scores aggregate into per-employee rankings, feed into performance reviews, bonuses beyond the suggestion bonus, or promotion. Vendors should design for "idea-level, not person-level" scoring and document the Art. 6(3) assessment.
- Even outside high-risk, GDPR transparency (Art. 13/15, C-203/22) and works-council demands make explainable scores (criteria, weights, reasons in plain language) a de facto requirement.
- Pseudonymous submission, where the assessor sees the idea and not the name until the decision, plus role-separated identity reveal for bonus payout, addresses both fairness (blind evaluation) and the Nr. 6 monitoring concern.
- Using submitted ideas or employee texts to train or fine-tune models will be a red flag. Contractual no-training clauses and opt-outs are expected.

### Gaps
- Final OJ publication date of the AI Omnibus and whether Council adoption completed were not verified.
- Whether Annex III point 4 wording itself changed in the Omnibus was not confirmed (sources mention only date shifts).
- Status of a German employee data protection act in late 2026 is unknown.

## Survey data on German / automotive employees' attitudes to AI at work

### Takeaway
German employees use AI widely: about 55-62% in KPMG and BIBB/IAB/ZEW data. They trust it less than people in most countries and are among the least AI-trained. Only about 20% have had AI training, and only 32% trust AI-generated information. Explicit fear of being fully replaced is a minority view (about 13-15%), but trust in AI's outputs and governance is low. No automotive-specific employee survey was found.

### Cited Findings
- KPMG / University of Melbourne (Germany report, May 2025, more than 1,000 respondents in Germany): 66% use AI privately, at work or at university; only 32% are prepared to trust AI-generated information; 20% have had AI training (39% globally); Germany ranks second-to-last of 47 countries on AI literacy; 37% see more benefits than risks vs 39% more risks; 62% say their company uses AI, 55% use AI tools themselves; almost half admit passing off AI content as their own or knowingly breaching internal rules; only 46% of companies have genAI guidelines. — [KPMG press release, 12 May 2025](https://kpmg.com/de/en/media/press-releases/2025/05/between-everyday-life-and-concern-two-thirds-of-germans-use-ai-but-only-a-few-trust-the-technology.html)
- KPMG/Melbourne workforce cut: about 42% of employees in Germany consciously use AI for their job, and trust in AI at work declined compared with 2022. Trust in Austria is 43% and in Switzerland 55%, against a 53% global average. The German workplace trust figure is not in the snippet. — [KPMG study page](https://kpmg.com/de/en/home/insights/2025/04/study-generative-ai-in-the-german-economy-in-2025.html) (via search snippet)
- Bitkom (Jul 2025, 1,005 respondents including 513 employed): about one fifth have been trained in AI at work, and 70% have received no AI training offer. — [Bitkom press release](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Ein-Fuenftel-im-Job-zu-KI-geschult)
- Bitkom on job fears: 14% fear AI could replace them completely (2025 write-up); a 2024 write-up gives 13%; 2026 reporting: 23% consider their own job largely replaceable by AI, and 29% think AI could replace their boss. 45% (2024) would like an AI personal assistant at work. Figures come from secondary write-ups and conflict slightly. — [Computerwoche](https://www.computerwoche.de/article/2833412/arbeitsplatz-ki-assistenz-oder-jobkiller.html); [retail-news.de 2026](https://retail-news.de/bitkom-ki-arbeitswelt-chefs-ersetzbar-2026/); [silicon-saxony](https://silicon-saxony.de/bitkom-ki-einsatz-bei-der-arbeit-beschaeftigte-sind-geteilter-meinung/)
- TÜV / Forsa: 15% fear no longer being needed because of AI. — [Hannover Messe news](https://www.hannovermesse.de/en/news/news-articles/german-employees-not-ecstatic-about-ai)
- BIBB/BAuA/IAB/ZEW (2025, DiWaBe 2.0): 62% of employees use AI, growth is driven by generative AI, and use is concentrated in office work. IG Metall works-council survey 2/2025: AI used in 27% of workplaces. — [Gerst slides](https://www.arbeitnehmerkammer.de/fileadmin/Veranstaltungen/Veranstaltungsarchiv/VAdoku_KI_und_Macht_Detlef_Gerst.pdf)
- Company adoption: Bitkom AI study 2025 found 36% of German companies use AI (20% a year earlier) and 47% plan or discuss it. HR use is 14%, production processes 20%. — [Bitkom guide citing Bitkom KI-Studie 2025](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)
- Automotive signals: VW gives more than 330,000 employees Copilot access (Oct 2026). Mercedes reportedly has about 60% of staff using AI daily (secondary, via search). — [drweb.de](https://www.drweb.de/volkswagen-microsoft-copilot-beschaeftigte/)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint is low trust plus low literacy, not unwillingness to use AI. A platform that explains itself, keeps humans visibly in charge, and teaches through use (e.g. showing why a score is what it is) fits the German profile.
- At OEMs facing job cuts, idea platforms that solicit "efficiency" ideas risk being read as self-rationalisation. Explicit guarantees, such as no idea leading to the submitter's own job loss and ideas not being used for performance appraisal, will matter to both employees and works councils.
- High tool exposure at VW and Mercedes means shop-floor and office staff already have reference points. Blue-collar production staff (lower desk AI use per BIBB) need non-desk channels.

### Gaps
- No automotive-only or OEM-only employee AI-attitude survey was found. No Deloitte, McKinsey, EY, Randstad or Gallup Germany-specific worker trust figure was retrieved in this pass.
- Gallup Germany Engagement Index AI questions were not checked.

## Fairness perceptions in idea evaluation and reward; perception of AI scoring; explainability expectations

### Takeaway
Classical Ideenmanagement fails on procedural justice: months-long waits and rejections without reasons demotivate. Research on algorithmic fairness is mixed and context-dependent. Algorithms are often seen as less fair for tasks needing human judgment, such as work evaluation. Human oversight helps but does not fully close the gap. Explainability is both a perceived-fairness lever and a legal requirement.

### Cited Findings
- IW Köln warns against soliciting suggestions and then leaving staff waiting for months, or rejecting without reasons, because both demotivate. Bonuses are seen as particularly effective. — [Deutsche Handwerks Zeitung citing IW Köln](https://www.deutsche-handwerks-zeitung.de/?p=154358)
- The dib study (older, 2012 data, 145 companies with about 1.6 million employees) found 164 suggestions per 100 employees in 2012, double the previous year. — [CIO.de](https://www.cio.de/article/3673755/in-den-mitarbeiterkoepfen-schlummern-milliarden-2.html) (old)
- Idea programmes lose ideas without clear ownership, documented decisions, status updates, feedback and formal closure. — [FHNW student project 2026](https://studierendenprojekte.wirtschaft.fhnw.ch/view/5189) (low authority)
- BAG 9 AZR 863/13 shows that disputes over eligibility (inside or outside job scope) and assessed savings reach the courts. Some BVs provide review by parity commissions. — [anwalt24](https://www.anwalt24.de/urteile/bag/2015-05-19/9-azr-863_13); [Betriebs-Berater](https://betriebs-berater.ruw.de/arbeitsrecht/nachrichten/Betrieblic-Vorschlagswe--ueberpruef-der-Entscheid-ein-paritaeti-besetz-Kommiss--Ausschl-des-Praemienanspru-wen-der-Verbesserungsvorsch-zu-den-Arbeitsa-28190)
- A systematic review (Starke et al.) finds human-vs-algorithm fairness comparisons inconsistent and strongly context-dependent. Human oversight raises perceived fairness but often stays below purely human decisions. Giving people outcome control raises fairness. — [arXiv 2103.12016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12016)
- Lee (2018): tasks requiring human skills, such as hiring and work evaluation, are seen as fairer when decided by humans. — [via Schettler, LU journal](https://journal.lu.lv/hssl/article/download/591/559/759)
- A review of selection contexts finds mostly negative effects of algorithmic decisions on fairness perceptions. — [IJSA (Wiley)](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/10.1111/ijsa.12417)
- Counterpoint: a 2024 scenario study found algorithms seen as fairer than managers, especially for unfavourable outcomes. — [Jing, Qiu & Ren, QJEM](https://www.jgcm.ac.cn/qjem/article/id/0c8a6d51-2676-4fd9-be51-b74f392ab0b6)
- Algorithms may violate procedural-justice norms by reducing people to quantified metrics, even while reducing bias (talk abstract). — [Fast, UCSB](https://tmp.ucsb.edu/node/414)
- Legal side of explainability: GDPR plus C-203/22 require comprehensible explanation, and the AI Act Art. 86 gives a right to explanation for high-risk decisions. — [Bitkom guide](https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-02/bitkom-leitfaden-kuenstliche-intelligenz-und-mitbestimmung.pdf)

### Inferences
- The AI's best fairness contribution is procedural: speed (instant acknowledgement and first feedback), consistency (same criteria for everyone), transparency (status tracking, reasons for every rejection) and voice (submitter can respond to or contest the AI's assessment).
- AI should *advise* assessors and committees, not decide rewards. Final bonus decisions should stay with humans or the parity commission defined in the BV, and the AI's contribution should be visible to the submitter ("AI suggested X because Y; assessor decided Z").
- Blind (pseudonymised) evaluation can be marketed as a fairness gain over manager-dependent evaluation, which addresses favouritism concerns.
- Show criteria and weights upfront, and agree them with the works council as part of the Nr. 12 Grundsätze.

### Gaps
- No German Ideenmanagement-specific study on perceived fairness of AI-assisted idea evaluation was found.
- Current dib / Zentrum Ideenmanagement figures (processing times, satisfaction) were not retrieved. Only 2012-era data was found.

## Data residency, on-prem vs cloud, TISAX and IT-security expectations at OEMs

### Takeaway
TISAX is the automotive baseline for any vendor handling OEM information. Assessment level AL2 covers "confidential" data and AL3 covers "strictly confidential" data, prototypes and special data. Idea submissions often contain unreleased product and process know-how, so OEMs will likely classify them at least confidential. Hyperscaler TISAX labels do not cover the vendor's own setup. No public OEM rule mandating on-prem for AI was found, but data sovereignty is a stated top requirement.

### Cited Findings
- TISAX levels per ENX: "Confidential", "High availability", "Test vehicles", "Proto events" and "Data" require AL2; "Strictly confidential", "Very high availability", "Proto parts", "Proto vehicles" and "Special data" require AL3. Labels are valid for 3 years. — [Secureframe TISAX guide](https://secureframe.com/de-de/blog/tisax)
- Hyperscaler TISAX labels apply only to the provider. They say nothing about the supplier's IAM, data classification or deployment, so the vendor must have its own assessment. — [Qovery](https://www.qovery.com/blog/tisax-requirements-automotive-suppliers-hyperscaler-cloud)
- Automotive cloud strategy requirements: security, compliance, low latency and data sovereignty (Jul 2026). — [NTT DATA](https://www.nttdata.com/de-at/insights/blog/clouds-vital-role-in-enabling-automotive-ai)
- Sovereign and TISAX cloud offerings: Outscale (TISAX plus SecNumCloud); Centron (ISO 27001 on IT-Grundschutz, BSI C5, documented TOMs). — [Outscale](https://go.outscale.com/tisax-cloud-automotive); [Centron](https://www.centron.de/en/branchen/automobilindustrie) (vendor marketing)
- Copilot-style enterprise AI stores prompts and outputs, which admins can view (Purview). Retention, admin access and over-broad permissions are flagged as BV topics. — [drweb.de](https://www.drweb.de/volkswagen-microsoft-copilot-beschaeftigte/)

### Inferences
- A vendor should expect: its own TISAX label (AL2 minimum, AL3 if prototype or strictly confidential content is in scope), EU-only hosting and LLM inference with no third-country transfer (or a sound Art. 46 basis), a no-training guarantee, customer-managed keys, SSO and role-based access, audit logs that the works council can inspect, and an option for single-tenant or on-prem / customer-VPC deployment. This matches NextUp's "one Docker stack per company, hosted VM or on-prem" plan.
- Using US LLM APIs directly will draw scrutiny. EU-region inference (e.g. Azure EU or AWS Bedrock Frankfurt) or self-hosted open-weight models are likely to be required options.

### Gaps
- OEM-specific supplier IT-security requirements for AI services (e.g. VW, BMW or Mercedes supplier portals, VDA ISA AI controls) are not public in what was found.
- No survey quantifying OEM on-prem vs cloud preference for HR or collaboration tools was found.
