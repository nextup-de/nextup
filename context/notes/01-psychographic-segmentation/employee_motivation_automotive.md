# Employee-side psychographics of idea / KVP / Ideenmanagement participation in large automotive companies

Scope: how shop-floor and office employees at OEMs / large Tier-1s think and feel about submitting improvement ideas, mapped to four traits — (A) Improvement Oriented Mindset, (B) Desire to be heard, (C) Recognition & Meaningful Impact, (D) Low Tolerance for extra work. Research date: 2 Nov 2026. Note: most company-level automotive figures that are publicly findable are 2011–2019 press releases; recent (2023–2026) OEM-specific numbers were NOT found in public sources (see Gaps). Older data is labelled with its year.

---

## Q1. What participation and implementation numbers do German automotive firms and Toyota report?

### Takeaway
German OEMs/Tier-1s run very large, mature, cash-premium suggestion systems (tens of thousands of ideas, EUR 60–125M yearly savings, EUR 8–21M yearly premiums, ~40–60 % implementation), but per-head volume in big German firms is low (~0.3 ideas/employee/year across large companies) versus Toyota's ~10–14 per person. The automotive and electrical industries are still the most active sectors in Germany. Public numbers are mostly 2011–2019; no OEM publishes a consistent recent series.

### Inferences
- Rough order-of-magnitude for German OEMs (2011–2019): ~0.3–0.6 submitted ideas per employee per year in Germany (e.g. Daimler ~77–79k ideas on a German workforce of roughly 170k; VW ~60k on a German workforce of ~120k+) vs Toyota ~14. Workforce denominators are my estimates, not sourced.
- Premiums run at roughly 7–17 % of reported savings (Audi 8.1/109 ≈ 7 %; VW 21.4/125 ≈ 17 %; Daimler 20/74.8 ≈ 27 %; Bosch 7.7/33 ≈ 23 %) — the "Prämie" is a material, expected part of the deal for German employees.
- Implementation ~42–59 % at German OEMs/Tier-1s → roughly every second idea is rejected, so the rejection experience (Q2) is a mass phenomenon.
- Decision latency in typical German large firms ≈ 75 days + 30 days to implementation; best-in-class is 14 days. That gap is the most obvious product opportunity.
- Toyota-style volume comes from a different model: many tiny, self-implemented ideas with small rewards and near-100 % implementation, vs the German BVW model of fewer, evaluated, savings-calculated ideas with large premiums.

### Gaps
- No 2020–2026 public figures found for VW, BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche, Audi, ZF, Schaeffler; Continental and Bosch only older figures. Sustainability reports (GRI "employee participation") and Betriebsrat newsletters would be the next place to look.
- Bosch page blocked (HTTP 403); Bosch figures are snippet-level and undated.
- Toyota primary source for 14.4 ideas/person (2023) not located; ASQ 2009 comparison (Japan 32.5 ideas/employee, 87 % implementation vs US 0.11, 32 %) appeared only in a search summary of an unidentified paper — treat as unverified.
- dib / Zentrum Ideenmanagement annual reports with automotive-sector breakdowns are not publicly accessible.

## Q2. Why do employees stop submitting? What does voice / silence research say?

### Takeaway
Employees stop because speaking up feels futile (no answer, ideas parked for months, "Schweigeprämien"), risky (being labelled a complainer, damaging the relationship with the supervisor, rationalising one's own or colleagues' jobs), or unfair (opaque criteria, inconsistent rewards). Silence is collective and contagious. Rejection itself is not the killer — silent or unexplained rejection is.

### Cited Findings
- Milliken, Morrison & Hewlin (2003, J. Management Studies; 40 interviews): the most frequent reason for silence was fear of being viewed/labelled negatively and damaging valued relationships; other reasons: feelings of futility, fear of retaliation, concern about negative impact on others, poor relationship with supervisor, organisational characteristics. 74 % of those uncomfortable raising an issue said colleagues who knew also felt uncomfortable (collective silence) — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jomstd/v40y2003i6p1453-1476.html); [full PDF copy](https://homepages.se.edu/cvonbergen/files/2012/12/AN-EXPLORATORY-STUDY-OF-EMPLOYEE-SILENCE_IISSUES-THAT-EMPLOYEES-DONT-COMMUNICATE-UPWARD-AND-WHY.pdf); [Psych Safety Field Guide](https://explore.psychsafety.com/n/milliken-morrison-hewlin-2003/)
- Futility as a silence driver (Milliken et al. 2003; Morrison & Milliken 2003) — [Kemmy Business School research bulletin 2018](https://ulsites.ul.ie/sites/default/files/business/kbs_research_bulletin_march_2018.pdf)
- Deichmann (RSM Erasmus), 1,800 suggestions from one company's online suggestion box over 12 years: rejection tends to bring people back, while people whose first idea was accepted were less likely to return; but there is a tipping point at ~27 rejected ideas after which people gradually stop. Recommendation: give focused feedback on criteria and avoid negative consequences for rejected submissions — [RSM Discovery 2014](https://rsm.nl/discovery/2014/rejection-motivates); [HR Magazine](https://www.hrmagazine.co.uk/content/news/rejection-can-motivate-employees-ideas)
- Deichmann recommends feedback within one week for small improvement suggestions — [WiWo](https://www.wiwo.de/erfolg/management/ideen-management-so-sorgen-mitarbeiter-fuer-innovationen/12232290.html) (via search summary)
- "Whoever never learns what happened to their suggestion will not submit a second one"; opaque selection criteria lead employees to simply stop submitting — [betrieblichesvorschlagswesen.de](https://www.betrieblichesvorschlagswesen.de/verbesserungsvorschlaege-gute-vorschlaege-foerdern-richtig-umsetzen/); [g-wie-gastro](https://g-wie-gastro.de/unternehmensfuehrung/verbesserungsvorschlaege/verbesserungsvorschlaege/)
- Employees stay silent because suggestions were not taken seriously or postponed endlessly — [Ideenmanagement per App, blupassion](https://blupassion.de/ideenmanagement-per-app/) (via search summary)
- Managers tend to attribute low participation to employee disinterest/resistance to change, whereas asking employees reveals other causes — [Gregor Stausberg, LinkedIn](https://de.linkedin.com/pulse/ideenmanagement-teil-1-gut-gemeint-reicht-nicht-gregor-stausberg)
- Heidelberg dissertation (Büch) on innovative behaviour in suggestion systems studies perceived justice as a driver of willingness to submit — [Univ. Heidelberg](http://archiv.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/volltextserver/11668/1/Dissertation_Veronika_Buech_Ideenmanagement.pdf); employees submit more when they believe their suggestions are taken seriously — [Uni Heidelberg press 2007](https://www.uni-heidelberg.de/presse/news07/2705mita.html)
- "Schweigeprämien" (paying a premium but never implementing the idea) are counter-productive; success factors: transparent, low-threshold process where employees know who evaluates and how premium is set; fast review with an interim message if a decision is delayed; visible appreciation from management; campaigns/"Ideensprints" — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Suggestion systems create problems when rewards are inconsistent or unpredictable — [Encyclopedia.com, Employee Suggestion Systems](https://www.encyclopedia.com/entrepreneurs/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/employee-suggestion-systems)
- Fear of rationalising one's own job: the most common operator fear about kaizen is losing their job after a kaizen event; cutting headcount right after an improvement dampens further kaizen — [shmula "Kaizen-ed Out of a Job"](https://www.shmula.com/kaizen-ed-out-of-a-job/404/); Toyota's stated principle (Imai, Nakao) that kaizen must not result in firing; freed capacity redeployed — [lean.org](https://lean.org/?p=4076); when lean is linked to job loss staff go quiet; in a no-layoff system the bigger fear was how one's own role changes — [Lean Blog 2025](https://www.leanblog.org/2025/11/lean-without-layoffs/); [Lean Blog 2025b](https://www.leanblog.org/2025/06/lean-is-about-growing-people/)
- Management support has a positive effect on participation rate and measurable benefit per employee — [HYPE Ideenmanagement-Studie 2023](https://www.hypeinnovation.de/blog/ideenmanagement-studie-2023)

### Inferences
- Trait (B) Desire to be heard is the dominant driver of churn: the system fails the employee at the "was I heard?" moment (2.5-month silence), long before money matters.
- Rejection is survivable — even motivating — if it is fast, explained and criteria-based; silent or opaque rejection triggers the futility→silence spiral.
- In the 2024–2026 German automotive context (restructuring, job-cut programmes at VW, Bosch, ZF, Continental, Schaeffler — general industry knowledge, not sourced here), the "I'm rationalising my own job" fear is likely amplified; productivity ideas need an explicit no-layoff / redeployment framing and works-council (Betriebsrat) backing. The design should let ideas be framed as safety, quality, ergonomics and frustration-removal, not only headcount savings.
- Supervisor relationship is a gate: if submission requires going through the Meister, fear of labelling bites; an option to submit directly (with optional anonymity toward the line) addresses it.

### Gaps
- No German automotive-specific survey quantifying the share of employees citing "slow feedback" vs "fear for job" vs "unfair premium" as reasons to stop. The FOM/HLP and dib studies may contain this but full text was not accessible.
- Detert & Edmondson (implicit voice theories) and Detert & Burris (leader openness) not retrieved in this pass.
- Deichmann original paper not retrieved; figures are from press coverage.

## Q3. What recognition works — Prämie vs visibility vs seeing implementation; team vs individual?

### Takeaway
In German automotive, cash premiums are the institutionalised norm (avg. ~EUR 1,400 per implemented idea, single ideas up to EUR 150k at Bosch, ~10–30 % of first-year savings), and field evidence shows rewards broaden participation and raise quality without crowding out intrinsic motivation. But recognition, transparent fair criteria and actually seeing the idea implemented are what keep people coming back; paying without implementing ("Schweigeprämie") backfires. Many large German ideas are submitted by pairs/teams.

### Cited Findings
- Average premium in surveyed German firms: "gut 1,400 Euro" per implemented idea; the scheme offers employees "Anerkennung und Prämien" — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Common premium rule of thumb: ~10 % of first-year savings, weighted by the idea's share; Bosch premiums up to EUR 150,000 — [Ärzteblatt / search summary](https://www.aerzteblatt.de/archiv/krankenhaeuser-die-ideen-der-mitarbeiter-nutzen-a4a01030-d79c-44a7-8c34-ed1c05b06b82)
- BMW premium level depends on creativity, quality improvement and economic efficiency of implementation — [bimmertoday 2020](https://www.bimmertoday.de/2020/02/24/bmw-cre8-mitarbeiter-ideen-sparen-uber-62-millionen-euro)
- Daimler examples are typically two-person submissions (Sindelfingen body-shop door bonding; Germersheim logistics); Mannheim waste-oil idea paid >EUR 65,000 to the employees — [mbpassion 2014](https://mbpassion.de/2014/03/ideenreichtum-der-mitarbeiter-spart-daimler-748-millionen-euro/); Audi example idea came from two maintenance technicians — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Gibbs, Neckermann & Siemroth (2014, Tinbergen DP 14-045; field experiment, >11,000 employees, 19 client teams, large technology company): rewards (points worth ~2.2 % of monthly net salary per accepted idea, plus up to 40 % of monthly net salary for top client rating) substantially increased idea quality and increased participation, but reduced ideas per participant (zero net effect on quantity). The broader participation base persisted after the reward ended (habituation); no evidence of motivational crowding out. Pre-existing motivators were intrinsic motivation, public recognition, career concerns — [Tinbergen Institute](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/14045.pdf)
- Rewards paid to idea-teams: in the same experiment, points for an accepted idea went to each member of the ideating team; client-rating points were split equally among ideators — [Tinbergen Institute](https://papers.tinbergen.nl/14045.pdf)
- Non-cash awards argued (practitioner view, 1992) to sustain interest better than cash — [Christian Science Monitor 1992](https://csmonitor.com/1992/1117/17082.html) (old, opinion)
- Inconsistent/unpredictable rewards cause problems — [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/entrepreneurs/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/employee-suggestion-systems)
- "Schweigeprämie" (premium paid but idea not implemented) is counter-productive — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Involving employees' ideas raises job satisfaction and loyalty — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Leaders matter as recognisers: Deutscher Ideenmanagement Preis has a "Best Leader" category (STIHL plant manager: 40 % participation; Wacker: 67 %) — [Zentrum Ideenmanagement 2025](https://www.zentrum-ideenmanagement.de/ideenmanagement-preistraeger-2025)
- Premiums are taxable wage income, a known pitfall in German Ideenmanagement — [Rödl & Partner, Apr 2023](https://www.roedl.com/insights/praemien-ideenmanagement-steuer-rechtliche-fallgrube/)

### Inferences
- Trait (C) Recognition & Meaningful Impact splits into two needs: (1) fair material reward (a German entitlement norm anchored in Betriebsvereinbarungen) and (2) seeing the idea live and being credited for it. A platform cannot replace (1) — it must integrate with the existing premium logic — but it can massively improve (2): status timeline, "your idea is now running on line 3", photos, thank-you from the Meister/plant manager, impact counters.
- Rewards reliably widen the base of first-time participants (Gibbs et al.), so a first-idea micro-recognition/badge is a cheap lever; quality is driven by rewarding acceptance, not submission.
- Team/pair submissions are the norm for large shop-floor ideas; platform must support co-authors with automatic share splitting, and team-level visibility (shift/line leaderboards) without public shaming of low-participation individuals.
- Recognition should come from the direct supervisor and from peers who benefit, not only from a central Ideenmanagement office.

### Gaps
- No German automotive study directly comparing cash vs non-cash recognition effects on repeat participation.
- Frese, Teng & Wijnen (1999) on suggestion-system motivation not retrieved.
- Current (2024–26) average premium per idea for specific OEMs not found.

## Q4. How much effort will employees tolerate — time to submit, form length, mobile / shop-floor access?

### Takeaway
Evidence points to a very low effort ceiling: "low-threshold" (niederschwellig) submission is a top success factor, participation collapses in large firms (0.3 ideas/employee vs 2.5 in Mittelstand), and blue-collar workers without PCs need mobile, terminal, QR or KVP-room access plus multilingual input. Continental's 22-language global system and mobile-first tenders show where big firms are heading. No hard data on acceptable minutes-to-submit was found.

### Cited Findings
- Success factor #1: a transparent, low-threshold process — employees can submit very easily and know who evaluates and how premium is set — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Large companies: 0.3 suggestions/employee/year vs 2.5 in Mittelstand — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Continental: 235,000 employees, ideas enterable in 22 languages; 450,000 suggestions in 2017 after new system rollout in 2016 — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- voestalpine (steel/automotive supplier), Aug 2025: office staff use the web app "myIDEA" on PCs; production staff without fixed PC workplaces got a dedicated access point in the KVP room — [TU Dresden PAL interview](https://pal.webspace.tu-dresden.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/250813_PAL_Interview-voestalpine.pdf)
- Industrial company tender (~5,000 employees): every employee worldwide must be able to submit via mobile devices, incl. workshop, warehouse, lab — [SoftGuide tender](https://www.softguide.de/ausschreibungen/industrieunternehmen-sucht-software-fuer-das-ideenmanagement)
- GEA Farm Technologies won 2025 "Safe & Healthy Work" award for a QR code on employee badges enabling 24/7 hazard reporting; Bayernwerk and DEHN winners used Power Automate / online forms — [Zentrum Ideenmanagement 2025](https://www.zentrum-ideenmanagement.de/ideenmanagement-preistraeger-2025)
- Ideas tend to come from production; idea management originates in manufacturing — [Computerwoche](https://www.computerwoche.de/article/2753008/in-den-mitarbeiterkoepfen-schlummern-milliarden.html)
- "Ideensprints" with teams released from daily work and methodological support raise participation — [iwd 2019](https://iwd.de/pdf/fortschritt-von-innen-421224)
- Ideas collected outside a central tool get lost; app-based submission prevents losing suggestions — [blupassion](https://blupassion.de/ideenmanagement-per-app/)
- Award winner Lehmann (Univ. Hohenheim, 2025) dissertation on web-based idea-management systems — [Zentrum Ideenmanagement 2025](https://www.zentrum-ideenmanagement.de/ideenmanagement-preistraeger-2025) (no findings extracted)

### Inferences
- Trait (D) Low Tolerance for extra work: the submit step must fit into a break or a line stop (target well under 2 minutes, voice/photo-first, pre-filled location/line/shift from a QR code at the station), with structuring (savings calculation, category, responsible department) done by AI or the evaluator, not the submitter. German BVW forms that demand a savings estimate push effort onto the employee and suppress small ideas.
- Shop-floor access: phone (often personal, so BYOD/no-login or badge-QR login), shared terminals in KVP/break rooms, and the Meister/team-lead submitting on behalf ("captured in the shift huddle"). Multilingual input is mandatory given migrant-heavy production workforces (Continental's 22 languages).
- Participation must not cost unpaid time: works-council sensitivity — ideas submitted during working time, no mandatory quotas.
- Follow-up effort matters too: employees will tolerate answering one or two clarifying questions if it visibly speeds the decision, but not re-filling forms.

### Gaps
- No empirical data found on acceptable submission time in minutes, form-length drop-off, or share of automotive blue-collar workers with company device access.
- No public OEM-specific info on mobile/app channels (e.g. VW, BMW, Mercedes internal apps).

## Trait profile summary (synthesis of the above, for the report writer)

Mapped to the four traits, with design implications for an idea platform (NextUp). These are inferences built on the cited findings above.

- **(A) Improvement Oriented Mindset** — Strong latent base: automotive/electrical are the most suggestion-active sectors in Germany; tens of thousands of ideas a year per OEM; Toyota shows ~14/person is possible when every small improvement counts. But in large German firms only ~29 % participate and 0.3 ideas/employee/year. *Design:* lower the "is this idea big enough?" bar (accept small kaizen-style ideas, quick-win track separate from premium-relevant BVW ideas); prompts tied to everyday pain points; campaigns/sprints.
- **(B) Desire to be heard** — The decisive retention driver. Silence comes from futility and fear of labelling; typical decision time is ~2.5 months; best-in-class is 14 days; researchers recommend feedback within a week for small ideas. *Design:* instant acknowledgement, named owner, visible status, SLA timers with automatic interim messages, reasoned rejections with criteria (rejection with feedback keeps people engaged), optional anonymity toward the direct line.
- **(C) Recognition & Meaningful Impact** — Cash premium is a German norm (avg ~EUR 1,400/implemented idea; ~7–27 % of savings at OEMs) and rewards raise participation and quality without crowding out; but paying without implementing backfires. *Design:* integrate with existing Prämien rules rather than replace them; show implementation evidence and impact; supervisor and peer recognition; co-author/team support with fair split; avoid "jobs saved" framing — show safety/quality/ergonomics impact.
- **(D) Low Tolerance for extra work** — Low-threshold submission is the #1 success factor; many workers have no PC; multilingual workforce. *Design:* mobile/QR/terminal, voice + photo input, AI structuring and duplicate detection, no mandatory savings calculation by the submitter, team-lead proxy submission, in working time.
