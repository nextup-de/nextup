## 1. What investors (EU/DACH and US) say in 2025–2026 about AI application moats

### Takeaway
Investors broadly agree that a "thin wrapper" (the core value is a call to a third-party model) is not investable on its own. They want evidence that the product is a wedge into a system of record, owns a workflow, builds proprietary or usable data, or controls distribution. Bessemer adds a twist: AI is weakening incumbent system-of-record moats such as integration cost and data lock-in. So an AI-native "system of action" can displace incumbents, but its own moat has to come from somewhere beyond integration lock-in.

### Cited Findings
- Bessemer, "Roadmap: AI systems of action" (Droesch & Patel, 29 May 2025):
  - Software is shifting "from systems of record to systems of action".
  - Systems of record (SoRs) in every category will be "completely reimagined" within 5–10 years. The authors call this a working thesis.
  - Incumbents' two deepest moats are (1) high implementation and customization cost and (2) control of critical data. AI weakens both: code generation may cut custom integration time by up to 90%, and AI-generated migration scripts lower switching costs.
  - Traits of likely winners:
    - a 10x "magic AI moment"
    - a wedge that runs alongside the incumbent first, then offers an exit path
    - access to adjacent labor or services budgets
    - a niche or vertical start
    - a downmarket start, moving up
  - Wedges can build proprietary data by passively capturing email, call and message activity.
  - SoR software spend is about $400B a year, plus $50B+ a year on maintenance and integration.
  - — [Bessemer Atlas](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/roadmap-ai-systems-of-action)
- Bessemer published a founder playbook, *Building Vertical AI* (January 2026). Per a secondary summary, it assesses vertical AI investments on four dimensions: functional value, economic value, competitive dynamics and defensibility. It frames vertical AI as competing for labor budgets rather than IT budgets. — [BVP PDF](https://www.bvp.com/assets/uploads/2026/01/BUILDING-VERTICAL-AI_PDF_BESSEMER_VENTURE_PARTNERS_BOOK_JANUARY_2026.pdf); summary via [dlsthoughts](https://dlsthoughts.substack.com/p/bessemers-vertical-ai-roadmap). The PDF was not opened, so treat the summary as secondary.
- Commentary on Bessemer's State of AI 2025: data moats are not about having more data but about making complex data usable. Insight Partners names workflow depth and proprietary data as the two main moat categories for vertical AI. — [Insight Partners](https://insightpartners.com/ideas/building-a-moat-in-the-age-of-ai) (search summary)
- Menlo Ventures' vertical AI thesis is titled "Software Finally Gets to Work". It frames vertical AI as software that does the work, i.e. takes labor budget. — [Menlo VC](https://menlovc.com/perspective/software-finally-gets-to-work-the-opportunity-in-vertical-ai/) (title and search context only, not fetched)
- An investor-facing post says it is cautious about fast-growing thin products. It wants evidence that a product is "a wedge into a system of record, marketplace". — [QED Investors blog](https://qedinvestors.com/blog/the-biggest-mistake-founders-are-making-in-ai) (search summary)
- Reforge: products that owned the systems of record in each generation (CRM, HCM, ERP; Siebel to Salesforce) built strong moats. "Systems of intelligence" are the new defensible layer. — [Reforge](https://www.reforge.com/blog/brief-why-systems-of-intelligence-are-the-new-defensible-moats)
- Sierra Ventures (Aug 2025): the next SaaS winners will be "systems of context". These capture unstructured signals and adapt, rather than acting as static systems of record. — [Sierra Ventures](https://www.sierraventures.com/content/systems-of-context)
- a16z podcast "Why AI Moats Still Matter (And How They've Changed)" (3 Dec 2025). Its teaser names becoming the system of record, network effects and deep customer embedding as moat sources. — [a16z](https://a16z.com/podcast/why-ai-moats-still-matter-and-how-theyve-changed/)
- A secondary Chinese-language summary of a16z guidance lists four paths:
  - become the system of record
  - lock in workflows
  - deepen vertical integration
  - build relationship moats
  - — [woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/ai/6235876.html). This is secondary; the original a16z text was not located.
- A VC glossary says a wrapper's defensibility "depends on ... how much proprietary data the wrapper accumulates". A commentator argues a real moat needs at least two of three: proprietary data, switching costs and a distribution channel. — [vcbeast](https://vcbeast.com/venture-capital-glossary/ai-wrapper); [hatchworks](https://hatchworks.com/blog/gen-ai/ai-wrapper-product-strategy/)
- Point Nine (per secondary 2026 newsletters) remains a SaaS and marketplace investor with AI much closer to the center. It is leaning into AI-linked B2B software rather than generic SaaS or thin wrappers. — [mean.ceo newsletter Aug 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=10105). This is a low-quality aggregator.

### Inferences
- NextUp's pitch has to answer the wrapper question directly. "LLM scores ideas" is a wrapper. What holds up is "the place where improvement decisions are recorded, owned, measured and audited in a works-council-compliant way across OEM and suppliers", i.e. a system of record for decisions plus a system of action (routing, measurement, Jira or project handoff).
- Bessemer's argument cuts both ways for NextUp. Integration depth into SAP, PLM or QMS (moat d) is a weaker moat than it was, because AI lowers integration cost for competitors too. Integrations remain necessary for adoption, but they won't hold off competitors. Outcome data (moat a) and compliance embedding (moat c) are harder to copy.
- "Labor budget" framing suits NextUp. The pitch can quantify the CI/lean-team hours and evaluation-committee time it replaces, not only software spend replaced, for example against HYPE licences.

### Gaps
- No primary 2025–26 moat essays were found from Cherry, Point Nine, Project A, La Famiglia, HV Capital, Speedinvest, UVC or b2venture. A targeted search returned only firm profiles. Next step: search each firm's blog directly (writing.pointnine.com, cherry.vc/news, uvcpartners.com/insights, speedinvest.com/knowledge).
- The original texts of Sequoia ("AI's $600B question", Cahn 2024) and NFX network-effects essays were not retrieved. Unverified background: NFX's classification lists data network effects as among the weakest kinds unless the data feeds back into a better product.

## 2. Pre-seed/seed benchmarks in Europe 2025–2026 and expectations for industrial or automotive enterprise AI

### Takeaway
European pre-seed valuations are roughly €2.4M (PitchBook Q1 2025 pre-money) to about $3.5M (Equidam Q1 2026). European seed pre-money is about €5.6M (PitchBook Q1 2025, secondary). Competitive European AI seed rounds are usually €2–4M. Traction bars have risen sharply: some "early-stage" investors now ask for £500k+ ARR at pre-seed or seed. A pre-revenue student team will therefore raise on pilots, paid PoCs and LOIs, or on non-dilutive money, not on ARR.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Realistic targets for NextUp: a pre-seed of about €0.5–1.5M at roughly €3–6M pre-money, or a seed of €2–4M once paid pilots exist. Given the PitchBook and Equidam medians, a student team without revenue should anchor near the European pre-seed median, not US caps.
- Rising ARR bars plus long automotive sales cycles imply two things. First, signal strength matters more than count. A paid PoC (even €20–60k) with a named Tier-1 or OEM, a written conversion path to a frame contract, and a works-council sign-off is worth more than many free pilots. Second, investors will discount unpaid "pilots".
- Tulip's Series D was led by a strategic (Mitsubishi Electric). In industrial AI, corporate VCs and strategics such as BMW i Ventures, Bosch Ventures, Porsche Ventures and Continental are a realistic investor class alongside financial VCs. This is an inference; the CVC names are not verified in this pass.

## 3. Which moat mechanisms are realistic for NextUp: evidence from comparable companies

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced patterns among comparables are:
- a university-born team that wins one marquee customer and partners with the dominant SoR vendor (Celonis with SAP)
- continuous-listening data owned at scale and then bought by the SoR (Peakon by Workday)
- a strategic investor or industrial distribution partner in manufacturing (Tulip with Mitsubishi Electric)

For NextUp, the strongest combination is (b) system of record for decisions, (c) compliance embedding and (f) reference customers in the early years. Proprietary outcome data (a) is the long-term moat once enough customers exist. Network effects (e) are speculative.

### Cited Findings
- Celonis, the closest DACH analogue (university spin-out selling process intelligence to large German companies):
  - Founded in 2011 by three TUM students who met through the student consultancy Academy Consult Munich. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celonis); [Silicon Allee](https://www.siliconallee.com/news/german-unicorn-celonis-secret-sauce-universities)
  - First customer was Bayerischer Rundfunk (a hotline improvement project). Sources disagree on when Siemens joined: 2011 per Contrary, 2014 per TUM. The founders got enterprise customers partly by writing directly to CEOs. — [Munich Startup](https://www.munich-startup.de/en/news/the-celonis-story-almost-too-good-to-be-true); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/celonis)
  - Self-funded for about the first five years. — [Contrary](https://research.contrary.com/company/celonis)
  - Joined SAP Startup Focus in 2012 and was the first company from that program to sign a reseller agreement with SAP (July 2015). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celonis)
- Workday acquired Peakon (Copenhagen, employee feedback) in January 2021 for about $700M in cash. Peakon was founded in 2014 and had raised about $68M. The rationale was "continuous listening" / real-time employee sentiment data. — [Workday newsroom](https://newsroom.workday.com/2021-01-28-Workday-Announces-Intent-to-Acquire-Peakon?asPDF=1); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/workday-acquires-peakon-for-700-million-to-capture-real-time-employee-sentiment-data/); [Enterprise Times](https://www.enterprisetimes.co.uk/2021/01/29/workday-to-buy-employee-engagement-firm-peakon/)
- Tulip's $120M Series D (Jan 2026, $1.3B) was led by Mitsubishi Electric, which will also roll out Tulip's composable AI for frontline operations. This is a strategic and distribution partnership. — [BuiltIn Boston](https://www.builtinboston.com/articles/tulip-raises-120m-series-d-1b-valuation-20260116); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/tulip-raises-120m-series-d-at-1-3b-valuation-to-scale-ai-for-frontline-manufacturing/)
- Bessemer: incumbents' moats of integration cost and data control are eroding. AI cuts integration build time by up to 90%, so integration depth alone is a weaker moat. Wedges that run alongside the incumbent and passively capture activity data can approach incumbent-level data. — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/roadmap-ai-systems-of-action)
- "Data moats aren't about having more data, but about making complex data usable." — Bessemer State of AI 2025 commentary via [Insight Partners / search summary](https://insightpartners.com/ideas/building-a-moat-in-the-age-of-ai)

### Inferences
Mechanism-by-mechanism assessment for NextUp (my synthesis):
- **(b) System of record for decisions: realistic and the core claim.** Today, decisions on improvement ideas live in email, Excel and the stage-gate meeting minutes of BVW (betriebliches Vorschlagswesen) and KVP (continuous improvement). Owning the auditable record (who decided, why, which evidence, which reward) is what Bessemer and a16z describe as an SoR wedge. Milestone: at one pilot site, 100% of idea decisions are recorded in NextUp, and the existing BVW tool or Excel is switched off.
- **(c) Compliance embedding: realistic and DACH-specific.** In Germany, a BVW system and any AI evaluating employees' submissions usually needs a works agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung) under co-determination rules. The EU AI Act adds transparency and logging duties. Once a works agreement is written around NextUp's process, replacing the tool means renegotiating it. That is a real switching cost. Its legal basis is not sourced here; it needs the legal/works-council research thread.
- **(a) Proprietary outcome data: strongest long-term moat, weak at pilot stage.** Its value grows only with measured outcomes (savings realized, implementation rate, time to decision) across many sites and customers. Peakon's $700M exit shows SoR vendors pay for longitudinal behavioral and sentiment data at scale. Milestone: a cross-customer benchmark such as "median time-to-decision for suppliers is X days" becomes possible at roughly 3–5 customers. Data-sharing consent must be designed in from day one.
- **(d) Integration into QMS, PLM and SAP: necessary for adoption, weak as a moat** (Bessemer: AI lowers integration cost for everyone). The Celonis lesson is about the partnership, not the integration: a reseller or partner status with SAP or a QMS vendor is a distribution moat. Aim for SAP Store / SAP partner status, or a partnership with a QMS vendor used in automotive.
- **(e) OEM–supplier network effects: speculative.** A plausible form is an OEM inviting suppliers into joint improvement or cost-down idea programs, a cross-company workflow. No comparable company was found that proved this, so treat it as upside, not a pitch pillar.
- **(f) Reference customers in a conservative industry: realistic and highest leverage early.** Celonis used early marquee names and Tulip took a strategic industrial investor. In automotive, one named Tier-1 reference plus VDA/TISAX-style security credibility shortens later sales cycles. TISAX is not sourced here.

### Gaps
- Not researched in this pass (searches not run or no results):
  - Personio, Leapsome, Culture Amp and Qualtrics moat histories
  - rready (Swiss innovation platform), Sideways 6, Workerbase
  - Unverified background: Qualtrics was bought by SAP (2018, ~$8B), then spun out and taken private by Silver Lake (2023, ~$12.5B). This would be useful as "experience data" SoR evidence and should be verified.
- No sourced evidence on works agreements or the AI Act as switching cost. Hand off to the legal research thread.
- Workerbase funding and status: no search results.

## 4. Exits and market size for idea/innovation management: is the category big enough?

### Takeaway
Classic idea/innovation management is a small, PE-consolidated niche. The HYPE + Planbox merger, backed by Main Capital (a buy-and-build buyout investor), targeted only about €30M combined revenue as one of the world's largest players. That is a PE roll-up market, not a venture-scale category. For venture investability, NextUp should position in a larger budget pool: continuous improvement and operational excellence, decision intelligence, or "labor budget" for CI teams. It can use idea management as the wedge.

### Cited Findings
- HYPE Innovation (Bonn) and Planbox merged on 27 Feb 2024, backed by Main Capital Partners. The combined company is described as one of the world's largest innovation management software providers with targeted turnover of almost €30M. Planbox adds North American and UK presence to HYPE's DACH base. — [Main Capital](https://main.nl/?p=9896); [HYPE news](https://www.hypeinnovation.com/news/hype-innovation-acquires-planbox-bolstering-leadership-in-strategic-innovation-management); [startbase](https://www.startbase.com/news/main-capital-partners-foerdert-fusion-von-hype-innovation-und-planbox)
- Main Capital bought a majority stake in HYPE in 2019 from Kennet Partners. Planbox is HYPE's fourth acquisition since then. Main specializes in buyouts and later-stage growth capital for buy-and-build. — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/news/dutch-investor-main-capital-acquires-majority-stake-in-german-software-company-hype-innovation/); [Drake Star](https://drakestar.com/our-work/hype)
- Qmarkets offers idea management, continuous improvement, trend management, technology scouting and innovation portfolio management. Brightidea was founded in 1999 in San Francisco. No 2024–2025 acquisition of either was found. — [CB Insights Qmarkets](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/qmarkets/financials); [Gartner peer reviews](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/innovation-management-tools/compare/product/brightidea-innovation-cloud-vs-qmarkets-impact-driven-innovation-software)
- Contrast at the adjacent "listening" layer: Peakon (employee feedback) exited at about $700M to Workday. The value was framed as real-time sentiment and productivity data feeding an HR SoR. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/workday-acquires-peakon-for-700-million-to-capture-real-time-employee-sentiment-data/)
- Bessemer: AI-native "systems of action" can address labor and services budgets, and SoR software spend is about $400B a year. — [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/roadmap-ai-systems-of-action)

### Inferences
- A category where the consolidated leader targets about €30M revenue implies a total market of perhaps a few hundred million euros for classic idea management software. That is too small to justify a VC return on its own. This is an inference; no market-size report was retrieved.
- Repositioning options that fit the evidence:
  - "Continuous improvement intelligence / decision system of record for operations". It ties to KVP/lean budgets and measurable savings, and sits next to Tulip-style frontline operations, which reached a $1.3B valuation.
  - "Employee voice to decision" layer. A Peakon-like data asset, sold to the COO/plant rather than to HR.
  - Keep "idea management" as the wedge product name for buyers (BVW/KVP owners), but not as the investor category.
- Exit logic for investors: strategic buyers would be SoR vendors that want the decision and outcome data, such as SAP, Siemens (Opcenter/Teamcenter), Dassault, PTC or QMS vendors. PE roll-ups like Main/HYPE are a floor, not the venture outcome. This is an inference; no such deal was found.

### Gaps
- Planview's acquisition of Spigit (unverified background: around 2020, via Planview's purchase of Spigit) was not confirmed in this pass.
- No Qmarkets or Brightidea ownership changes were found.
- No sourced market-size estimate (TAM) for innovation management software or for CI/operational excellence software. Gartner, Forrester or analyst numbers are needed.
- Exact HYPE revenue and deal multiples were not disclosed.

## 5. Public non-dilutive funding (DE / Berlin / CH)

### Takeaway
A CODE University team can realistically stack several programs:
- EXIST Gründungsstipendium: one year of personal stipends plus material costs and coaching
- Berliner Startup Stipendium: €2,000 per person per month for up to 18 months
- later IBB Pro FIT Frühphase: up to €500k
- Innosuisse coaching vouchers, if a Swiss entity is used (up to CHF 10k Initial and CHF 50k Core)

These can fund 12–18 months to a paid pilot without dilution. Exact current rates must be checked against the current guidelines.

### Cited Findings
- EXIST Gründungsstipendium:
  - Monthly stipends: €1,000 for students, €2,500 for graduates, €3,000 for PhDs (€2,000 for technical staff or vocational training)
  - Up to 3 people, for up to 12 months
  - Material costs up to €10k for an individual founder or €30k for a team, plus up to €5k coaching
  - The stipend is not tax-free
  - Sources cite 2016–2023 guidelines, so 2025/26 rates need checking
  - — [Förderdatenbank](https://www.foerderdatenbank.de/FDB/Content/DE/Foerderprogramm/Bund/BMWi/exist-gruendungsstipendium.html); [gruenderplattform](https://gruenderplattform.de/finanzierung-und-foerderung/exist-gruendungsstipendium); [EXIST monitoring report 2025](https://exist.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/EGS_Monitoringbericht_2025.pdf)
- Berliner Startup Stipendium (BSS): up to 18 months at €2,000 per person per month, with coaching and infrastructure via partner universities and incubators. Funded by Land Berlin plus ESF+. A new round started in March 2026. — [berlin.de](https://www.berlin.de/sen/wirtschaft/gruenden-und-foerdern/gruendungs-und-startup-foerderung/finanzielle-foerderung/zuschuesse/); [Senate press release 18 Mar 2026](https://www.berlin.de/sen/web/presse/pressemitteilungen/2026/pressemitteilung.1653813.php). This conflicts with the [Förderdatenbank](https://www.foerderdatenbank.de/FDB/Content/DE/Foerderprogramm/Land/Berlin/berliner-startup-stipendium.html), which gives up to €2,500 per month for 6–36 months; berlin.de is newer.
- IBB Pro FIT Frühphasenfinanzierung: up to 100% of eligible costs, max €500k for both phases combined, of which up to €200k is Frühphase 1. Pro FIT project financing was revised at the start of 2026, with a maximum grant of €500k. — [Förderdatenbank Pro FIT](https://www.foerderdatenbank.de/FDB/Content/DE/Foerderprogramm/Land/Berlin/pro-fit-fruehphasenfinanzierung.html); [IBB Förderfibel 2026/27](https://www.ibb.de/media/dokumente/publikationen/wirtschaft-in-berlin/foerderfibel/foerderfibel-2026-2027-barrierefrei.pdf)
- Innosuisse Start-up Coaching, which needs an innovative, science-based idea:
  - Initial Coaching: voucher up to CHF 10,000 over up to 12 months
  - Core Coaching: up to CHF 50,000 over up to 36 months
  - Up to CHF 130k in total across Initial, Core and Scale-up
  - Innovation Booster is a separate networking/partner instrument, not a coaching grant
  - — [Innosuisse Initial](https://www.innosuisse.admin.ch/en/initial-coaching-for-start-up); [Innosuisse Core](https://www.innosuisse.admin.ch/en/start-up-coaching-for-start-up); [Liechtenstein gov](https://www.llv.li/en/company/foundation-leadership/start-up-scene/start-up)
- b2venture maintains an overview of support programs as an alternative financing option. — [b2venture resources](https://resources.b2venture.vc/fundraising/alternative-financing-options/overview-of-support-programs)

### Inferences
- Sequencing for NextUp:
  1. EXIST or BSS (choose by eligibility; they generally can't be combined for the same people at the same time, which needs checking) while running the first pilot.
  2. Pro FIT Frühphase 1 (up to €200k) once a GmbH exists, to fund productization.
  3. Pre-seed equity after a paid PoC.
- Non-dilutive money is also an investability signal in DACH, since EXIST is selective. It lets the team reach the paid-PoC milestone before pricing a round near the European pre-seed median.
- Innosuisse is only relevant if a Swiss entity or Swiss research partner is used, e.g. via the SwissEduSolution link. It's a small amount, so it's mostly a credibility play.

### Gaps
- EXIST eligibility for CODE University (a private, state-recognized university) and the 2026 guideline details are not verified. EXIST Forschungstransfer and the newer "EXIST-Women"/reform details were not researched.
- Whether EXIST and BSS can be combined is not sourced.
- EU programs (EIC Accelerator, which needs a later TRL; Horizon) and BMBF/KMU-innovativ were not researched.
